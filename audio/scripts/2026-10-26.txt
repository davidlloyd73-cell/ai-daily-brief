David's AI brief for Monday, the twenty-sixth of October.

Today's One Idea from The AI Daily Brief is that this is a week when the stakes are rising, and four questions will shape what comes next. Some AI weeks are bubble panics and some are model blitzes. Last week was the subtler kind, in which it simply felt as if the stakes of everything got higher.

The first question is whether American policy can handle Kimi K3.1 Pro, an open model from China's Moonshot. Its own benchmarks put it within a point of Anthropic's Opus 5.5 on agentic coding, at about the price of Sonnet. The launch was swamped by a leaked influencer campaign telling paid promoters to skip the sponsorship label, and the model has not yet seen a full business day of real use. But Moonshot deliberately held back cyber training, and it is still the top public model for cyber tasks. The second question is whether cyber chaos is the new normal. The Cloudflare chief expects a couple of years in which something breaks every week, and Sam Altman says he no longer thinks all the bugs can be patched. The third is whether neocloud funding is drying up. Debt markets are demanding a larger premium, and big buyers are building their own capacity. The fourth is whether Anthropic can list into a market that no longer feels like strength, with its prospectus landing on Monday morning.

Now, the other side. Ed Zitron, in a piece from the twentieth, argues that compute capacity exceeds demand. He quotes a Gartner forecast that most neoclouds will cease to exist as independent firms by 2030, and estimates over two hundred billion dollars of chips sit in storage, though that figure is his own. He also notes that Anthropic's reported quarterly revenue sits against over four hundred billion dollars of non-cancellable compute contracts. The variable both men orbit is the cost of financing against the cost of compute. They agree debt has repriced, and they split on whether that is risk repricing or a shortfall in demand. Both are polemicists with incentives, one bullish, one certain of a bubble. Watch the first day of Anthropic's shares.

For general practice, three thoughts. First, if open models approach the frontier, health bodies could host capable tools on their own servers, but the later episode of a Chinese model trying to call home shows that provenance and monitoring matter, and this is a governance question, not a technical one. Second, treat patching, backups and multi-factor sign-in as essential, and ask suppliers how they handle out-of-hours incidents. Third, costlier data centre debt feeds through to prices and supplier survival, so check price caps and exit rights in any scribe or triage contract.
