David's AI brief for Friday, the twenty-third of October.

Today's One Idea from The AI Daily Brief is that every AI bubble signal is a proxy fight for one question, which is demand. Warehoused chips, distressed bonds, pension fund exposure and frozen share offerings each have their own logic, but they all collapse into whether AI demand will exceed what is being spent on infrastructure.

The host takes the main worries one at a time. Amazon reportedly has about five billion dollars of NVIDIA racks staged waiting for power, with roughly three billion more arriving each month. Critics call that proof of soft demand, but he argues it is a power problem, because nobody orders tens of billions of chips to leave them in a warehouse. Data centre debt, he says, is not being hidden the way subprime mortgages were, since the tenants are among the most creditworthy companies in the world. The neoclouds, the smaller firms renting out chips, are where the fear is concentrated. A listing in Australia was cancelled, and some CoreWeave debt traded at distressed levels. But the industry keeps backstopping them, and the host lists three signals that would really be alarming, beginning with a neocloud in trouble that nobody rescues. The most serious signal, he says, is revenue concentration. A fifth of Anthropic's first-half revenue came from two customers, and eighty per cent of the top spenders in one data set are venture-backed start-ups. He ends by noting that bubble calls have been wrong every year, though they deserve salt. The Anthropic listing will replace speculation with audited quarterly answers.

Now, the other side. Ed Zitron, in a piece from the twentieth, argues the boom needs revenue, debt and venture money to keep growing at once. He says nine AI-linked stocks make up nearly half the Nasdaq 100, that Amazon's stored chips show supply exceeding demand, and he cites Gartner's prediction that most neoclouds will not survive as independent firms. The variable both men orbit is demand. They agree the labs lean on a few big customers, and they split on whether idle chips mean weak demand or slow power. Both are polemicists with incentives, one bullish, one certain of a bubble. The signal is their shared worry about concentration. Watch the first audited quarter.

For general practice, three thoughts. First, a stock market correction would hit private pensions many clinicians hold, but not NHS funding directly. Second, AI scribes and triage tools are often built on venture-funded start-ups that depend on a few labs, so insist on data export, exit terms and a fallback in any contract. Third, Microsoft has cancelled an Essex data centre over grid delays, so UK-hosted AI may stay scarce and costly for some time.

That's the brief — source, The AI Daily Brief.
