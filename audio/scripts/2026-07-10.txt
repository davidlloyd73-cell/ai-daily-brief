David's AI brief for Friday, the tenth of July.

The one idea today: the coding playbook has become the knowledge-work playbook — and the race between the labs has moved from raw capability to cost. OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Work, an agent that takes a goal, connects to your files and apps, and stays on a project for hours — reconciling spreadsheets, chasing leads, building reports. Their own finance team says it cut month-end close from days to hours. Alongside it came a new three-model family: a flagship, a mid-size, and a small cheap one — and for the first time, OpenAI led its launch not with benchmark scores but with performance-per-dollar charts. The new flagship lands within a whisker of the best model available at a third of the cost. And Meta stunned everyone with a model competitive with the frontier at a tenth of the price — it built a working Minecraft clone in five minutes for seventy-three cents. Every model released this week led its pitch with cost.

Now, the other side. Ed Zitron, publishing the very same day, argues the real cost story runs in the opposite direction: AI is making everything dearer. One chipmaker now buys roughly two-thirds of the world's high-bandwidth memory, three companies control the memory supply, and the result is what he calls the largest rise in consumer electronics prices in history — dearer laptops, phones and games consoles for everyone. Both men are working from true numbers: tokens are getting cheaper while the hardware underneath gets dearer. The thing to watch is whether those bargain model prices hold through the year as memory costs bite.

For the surgery, three thoughts. First, an agent that reconciles spreadsheets and drafts reports describes a good deal of practice back-office work. If the optimists are right, this becomes as routine as the scribe; if the sceptics are right, the tempting price is temporary. Either way: nothing patient-identifiable near it without a proper impact assessment, and pilot on rotas and invoices first. Second, a revealing detail from the week — a large company switched AI suppliers purely over data retention. Retention terms are now a competitive lever, which means a practice can demand the same answers: how long is audio kept, where do the corrections go, and does your data train their models? Get it in writing. Third, the two cost stories both land on the practice budget: software licences should get cheaper as models do, but the same boom is pushing up the price of the computers on your desks. If you're planning an IT refresh this year, budget for dearer devices even as the clever software comes down in price.
