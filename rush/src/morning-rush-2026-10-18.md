## 1. MHRA's 2030 strategy puts AI in the pharmacovigilance chair

The MHRA published its 2030 strategy on 14 October. It commits to using AI to spot medicines safety signals earlier, working with the Health Data Research Service for real-world data, and to a new Centre for AI and Digital Health that should tell developers what regulators expect. An AI medicines development action plan follows in 2027. Yellow Card is promised a more conversational interface, including through the NHS App, and patient information should be structured digital content by 2030. The Royal College of Pharmacy welcomed it but stressed accountability and meaningful human oversight. No budget or headcount figures were published.

**The view from the surgery:** Yellow Card is the scheme I have always meant to use more and rarely did, because it was fiddly at 6.30pm. Making it easier to report is worth more than any algorithm, though an algorithm sifting the reports is welcome. Funding is the missing line.

## 2. Brown fat, depression and losartan: a hypothesis, not a prescription

An AI analysis of more than 101,000 patients linked detectable brown fat to lower rates of several cardiometabolic conditions and also of anxiety and depression. It flagged losartan as a candidate worth studying. Its effect on human brown fat has not been tested in clinical trials.

**Why it matters:** This is a hypothesis-generating exercise built on patients who happened to be scanned, with all the confounding that implies. Interesting for the researchers, irrelevant to Monday's clinic. Do not expect a patient to ask for losartan for their mood.

## 3. One man, one chip, one word

Science Corp said on 13 October that a patient with Stargardt disease received its 2mm PRIMA retinal implant in Sydney in May. At his first rehab session he saw a letter, and by the second he had read a word. The image is black and white and narrow, and the company hopes for near-normal sharpness within five years. These are company-reported results from a single patient.

**The view:** A genuine moment for him, and not yet evidence. Wait for a series, independent assessment and a sensible account of the failure cases before anyone says "cure".

## 4. Regulators close in on the frontier labs

The FTC sent civil investigative demands, each over 50 pages, to OpenAI and Anthropic on 15 October, covering security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy. A day earlier eight US states sued OpenAI in state court, alleging it misled parents about ChatGPT's safety for children. On 14 October the EU AI Office also sought information from both companies about this summer's agent incidents, after finding voluntary disclosure insufficient. The FTC step is an investigation, not a finding.

**Why it matters:** Three jurisdictions asking about incidents in one week suggests the voluntary-disclosure era is ending. It echoes the MHRA's incident-database idea in item one: the question everywhere is who has to tell whom when it goes wrong.

---

## 5. Fewer juniors through the door

Anthropic's report on 16 October says junior staff fell from 21% to 14% of its technical employees since early 2025, and from 27% to 9% of technical hires, although it says company growth also played a part. Separately, the NYT notes that office and administrative support is about one in ten US jobs, and over 86% of the roughly six million workers most at risk are women. The researcher quoted expects the damage to show as fewer hires rather than layoffs. Kroger is reported to be cutting about 3,000 corporate roles over three years.

**The view from the surgery:** Nobody is sacked; the bottom rung simply disappears. Medicine has its own version: trainees learn by doing the tasks the scribe and the triage tool now absorb. Worth asking where the next generation of experienced hands comes from.

### From the Eye

**Item one** Accenture is reported to be capping Claude spending at $29 per employee per month, after some staff apparently ran up as much as $1,000; the productivity revolution meets the expenses department. **Item two** Bank of America has brought in about 1,000 outside contractors this year to patch software flaws surfaced by advanced AI models including Anthropic's Mythos, on top of roughly 3,000 existing remediation staff: the machine finds the holes and humans do the plumbing. **Item three** OpenAI's advertising is reported to have reached about 60,000 advertisers, up from around 20,000 in late August, with some agencies claiming clicks at roughly $5 against $30 to $50 on Google; early agency numbers, so treat with the usual scepticism, but the chatbot is clearly becoming a shop window.
