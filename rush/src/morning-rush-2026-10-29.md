## 1. AMIE meets 98 real patients, and nobody had to pull the plug

A feasibility study in *The Lancet* (8 October) put Google's AMIE conversational AI in front of 98 patients at Beth Israel Deaconess in Boston, chatting from home before urgent primary-care appointments, with a physician watching every conversation live. None of four predefined stop criteria was triggered. AMIE's differential contained the doctor's final diagnosis 90% of the time, and it matched primary care physicians in blinded comparison, though doctors did better on practical, cost-conscious management. There was one hallucination, one interaction rated somewhat harmful, and fewer than half of patients felt their information would stay confidential. It was single-site, volunteers only, no comparison arm, excluded pregnancy, mental health and emergencies, and was funded by Alphabet.

**The view from the surgery:** A supervised history-taker is a more sensible ambition than a diagnostician, and anyone who has run a triage line knows the history is half the work. But the exclusions are the entire Monday morning. Promising, and still a long way from the front door.

## 2. The money keeps being spent

Alphabet reported Q3 revenue of $127.6bn, up 25%, with Google Cloud up 103% to $30.7bn and the Gemini app at a billion users; it lifted 2026 capex guidance to $195-205bn. Microsoft's quarter showed Azure up 48% and a filing putting OpenAI-related revenue at roughly $10bn, close to 11% of its total. Google also launched Gemini 4 Carbon. These figures come from one aggregator's digest of the company releases, so check them before quoting.

**Why it matters:** The same two or three suppliers sit underneath most of what the NHS will be sold, and a customer worth 11% of someone's revenue is not a customer who walks away easily. Procurement leverage matters more than the model of the month.

## 3. Who is measuring the slow harm?

In *STAT* (26 October), Cornell researchers argue that chatbot safeguards target acute harm inside a single message or session, while the cumulative effect of AI quietly displacing human contact goes largely unmeasured. Early studies link heavy companion-chatbot use with less socialising and more loneliness, though the evidence is preliminary and disputed. They want shared research instruments and third-party evaluation. No new data, an argument rather than a finding.

**The view:** Loneliness already fills our appointment books, usually under another label. If a tool is quietly adding to it, the first people to see it will be GPs, long before anyone publishes.

## 4. The Budget small print

Coverage of yesterday's Budget points to £290m for winter pressures and robotic surgery alongside the productivity and AI review, with sovereign AI funding also mentioned. I have only seen headlines and trade summaries, not the Treasury documents, so treat the figures as indicative.

**The view from the surgery:** Winter money announced in late October is a forecast of winter, not a response to it. Robotic surgery is a hospital story; primary care will be offered a pathfinder and a webinar.

---

## 5. Adverts arrive in the answers

Google is extending AI Mode adverts to ten more countries and adding multi-turn ads in the US. The reported logic is simple: if people ask questions in conversation, the advert can join the conversation too.

**Why it matters:** Patients are already asking these assistants about their symptoms. Whoever pays for placement in that answer is now, in some small way, in the consulting room. Worth asking which product your patients mean when they say "Google told me".
