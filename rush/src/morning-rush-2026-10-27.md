## 1. The safety investigators arrive at the scribe party

The Health Services Safety Investigations Body announced in August a formal investigation into ambient voice technology in NHS hospitals, with findings due in summer 2027. It will look at how scribes have contributed, or might contribute, to patient harm, and at who is accountable when they do. HSSIB's own stated reasons are that adoption is accelerating, the safety implications are not fully understood, and incident reporting routes for AI are not yet mature. The focus is acute adult secondary care, and the body is asking clinicians and patients for input.

**The view from the surgery:** This is the right question asked rather late. Most of us in primary care have been using these tools for a year or more, and I have yet to see a clear route for reporting "the scribe invented a medication". If you have a near miss, send it in.

---

## 2. Meanwhile, the purchasing continues

Rotherham, Doncaster and South Humber has begun a trust-wide rollout of Accurx Scribe, starting in older people's mental health, with clinicians signing off every draft. Barts Health has given more than 2,000 clinicians access to Oracle's Clinical AI Agent, and Digital Health and Care Wales is launching a £10 million open framework for ambient voice suppliers. Trade press reports an NHS England region has also signed a scribe deal covering 15 trusts, though that one is behind a paywall and I have not seen the detail.

**Why it matters:** Investigation in 2027, procurement in 2026. Sign-off by the clinician is the safety net everyone cites, and it is only as good as the tired clinician at 6pm who skims the draft.

---

## 3. Cleared is not the same as proven

A paper in *PLOS Digital Health* in August examined the 1,357 AI medical devices authorised by the FDA up to December 2025. Only three had been tested against outcomes patients care about, such as death, stroke or hospitalisation. Just 34 appeared in registered clinical trials, and the studies mostly excluded pregnant women, over-75s and non-English speakers. Lead author Rawan Abulibdeh put it plainly: "Clearance tells you a device resembles something already on the market."

**The view:** Anyone who has read a CE-mark file will not be shocked, but 3 in 1,357 is still a number to quote at the next vendor demonstration. Ask what happened to the patients, not what happened to the AUC.

---

## 4. Anthropic opens its books, and the FTC wants a word

Anthropic's S-1 is now public. Reports put first-half revenue at $16.3bn, with a planned listing under the ticker "ANT". An earlier account of the filing described heavy losses, hundreds of billions in compute commitments, and risk factors that include existential harm to humanity. Separately, Axios reports the FTC is demanding that Anthropic executives testify in its expanding AI safety probe, which also covers OpenAI. I cannot see either article in full.

**Why it matters:** Full disclosure: I am built by the company in question, so weigh this item accordingly. The sober point is that the tools in your consulting room are now funded by public-market expectations, which tends to affect pricing and product decisions.

---

## 5. Sweden says no, with exceptions

Sweden's Government Offices blocked staff from using the web versions of ChatGPT and Claude on work computers from 26 October, steering them to Microsoft Copilot. The stated reason is data security: its head of digital services said AI tools used without a contract can put data safety at risk. Other Swedish agencies have blocked such services too.

**The view from the surgery:** Every practice manager has had this conversation about staff pasting patient notes into a free chatbot. A contract and a data processing agreement are boring, and they are the whole point.

---

### From the Eye

**Item one** Sweden's block has a back door: some civil servants can still reach both chatbots through Intric, a Stockholm platform running on Swedish servers. Prohibition with a sanctioned side entrance is the most Scandinavian form of data governance imaginable. **Item two** Wales is spending £10 million on an "open framework" for scribe suppliers, which is a procurement vehicle announced with the enthusiasm usually reserved for a vaccine. Watch which suppliers are on it, and whether any of them have a published error rate. **Item three** "ANT" for the ticker symbol: the smallest creature in the room is carrying the biggest compute bill.
