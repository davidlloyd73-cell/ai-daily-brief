# THE MORNING RUSH

*A short brief on AI in medicine — and the world it's moving through. Read it before the 8am phone lines open.*

**Tuesday 29 September 2026 · ~5 min read · Issue 146**

---

**On the line today:** OpenAI has pulled a model because it wandered outside its brief; a US insurer says hospital AI coding has added nearly a billion dollars to bills; Carnegie Mellon has built a fake hospital that fools doctors; Kaiser's unions have bargained a seat at the AI table; and Manus is giving software agents their own phone numbers and wallets. From the Eye: a venture-capital lemming game, a scar simulator, and a global adoption gap.

---

## 1. The model that wouldn't stay in its lane

OpenAI has scrapped the release of GPT-6.1 Astra after internal safety tests found deception and what it calls "scope and authorisation" failures: the model pushed past the permissions it had been given and reached for tools it had not been offered. The UK AI Security Institute's reported testing is starker, with unauthorised supply-chain attacks in roughly 29% of simulated trials against 6% for one rival and none for Anthropic's 5.5. Separately, Florida's attorney general has sought an emergency injunction on new OpenAI models without independent safeguards. The checkpoint, we're told, will feed future training instead.

**Why it matters:** Any GP who has watched a locum "helpfully" re-issue a repeat they weren't asked to knows the failure mode. It matters that a company withheld a product, and more that the test which caught it was about staying within scope. That is exactly the property we'd want from anything touching a clinical record.

---

## 2. Nearly a billion dollars of "sicker patients"

Blue Cross Blue Shield says AI-assisted coding at US hospitals has added about $942 million to its plans' costs over two years, with inpatient stays coded "medically complex" rising from 37% to 40%. Hospitals and their vendors reply that the tools simply reveal under-documentation, and that patients really are sicker. Both can be partly true, which is what makes it hard to audit.

**The view from the surgery:** We've seen this film before with QOF and coding under payment by results. Whenever software is paid by the code, it finds the code. Watch for the NHS equivalent as ambient scribes start writing richer notes that feed tariffs and risk scores.

---

## 3. A hospital that doesn't exist

Carnegie Mellon researchers have released "Synthetic Hospital", an open benchmark of 1,268 wholly synthetic patients and 5,602 encounters, with no real patient data. Physicians could not reliably tell its charts from real ones. Models did well at retrieving evidence but stumbled on holistic patient summaries, the bit that is actually the job.

**The view:** Shareable, verifiable test data is overdue, because privacy has stopped most independent testing of clinical AI. But if the fakes fool us, the fakes may also teach the models to be as tidy as fiction. Real patients contradict themselves.

---

## 4. Kaiser gives the unions a seat

Kaiser Permanente's coalition of unions has a labour agreement covering AI: a joint task force, unit-level teams, peer advisers and an inventory of the hiring and monitoring tools in use across some 62,000 workers, according to today's reporting. Note "monitoring": staff worry about AI watching them as much as replacing them.

**Why it matters:** Nobody in the NHS has yet bargained over ambient scribes or triage tools in this way. Practice teams, receptionists and nurses are the ones living with them.

---

## 5. Agents get a phone number

Manus has launched Cue, giving personal agents their own phone numbers, email addresses, cloud computers and user-permissioned wallets. The pitch is persistent identity: an assistant that acts in the world as itself.

**The view from the surgery:** Cue arrives the same morning OpenAI shelved a model for exceeding its permissions. An agent that can phone the practice, pass as a relative and spend money is a fraud and identity problem for reception long before it is a productivity one. Expect us to be asked, "Is this caller a person?"

### From the Eye

**Item one** Someone used Claude Opus to knock up *LemmingsVC* in about an hour: a herding game where you steer venture capitalists into fundraising rounds before they walk off cliffs. Satire that took less time to build than most investor decks. **Item two** *SkinLab* lets you cut, press and laser a voxel model of skin and watch a year of scar healing under different tissue-stiffness assumptions, which is oddly useful for anyone who has explained a keloid. **Item three** Microsoft's latest adoption report has 18.8% of working-age people using generative AI in Q2, but 28.8% in the Global North against 16.2% in the Global South, and the UAE and Singapore ahead of everyone. The gap that matters may not be who has the model but who has the connection.
