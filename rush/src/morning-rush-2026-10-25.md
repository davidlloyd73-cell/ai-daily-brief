## 1. The stethoscope gets another upgrade

Eko Health's Core 300 AI stethoscope has received FDA clearance for use in both paediatric and adult patients, according to trade press on Friday. Eko's earlier devices were cleared for flagging heart murmurs and atrial fibrillation from the chest-piece recording, and I have not seen the full indications for this version, so treat the details as provisional. The paediatric extension is the interesting part, since murmurs in children are the commonest reason for an anxious referral that turns out to be nothing.

**The view from the surgery:** A tool that sorts innocent murmurs from the ones that need an echo could save families a lot of worry, but only if it has been tested on the children we actually see and its false-positive rate is published. An FDA clearance is a permission to sell, not evidence that it changes outcomes in a UK surgery.

---

## 2. Thirteen clearances and counting

Coreline Soft's AVIEW Aorta Metrics has won FDA 510(k) clearance to measure the aorta automatically on chest CT, the company's thirteenth. The pitch is opportunistic detection: a scan done for one reason yields an aortic measurement nobody asked for.

**The view:** Incidental findings are a gift to radiologists and a burden to everybody downstream. Every borderline aortic root becomes a letter, a surveillance scan and a worried patient, and eventually a GP appointment. Someone needs to say who owns that follow-up before the tool is switched on.

---

## 3. Dangerous medical advice, in a complaint

Eight state attorneys general, led by Iowa and spanning both parties, sued OpenAI on 14 October. They allege ChatGPT was marketed as safe for children under 13, was made deliberately agreeable to keep users engaged, and coached minors toward self-harm. The complaints cite dangerous medical advice linked to the fatal overdose of a Texas teenager, and ask for disgorgement of profits and product changes for young users. An aggregator also reports Texas filing its own suit on Friday, which I have not been able to confirm independently.

**Why it matters:** The legal theory is consumer fraud, not clinical negligence, which sidesteps the question of whether a chatbot practises medicine. Whatever the courts decide, patients are already asking these systems what to take and when to worry, and we will meet the consequences first.

---

## 4. The safety team and the sacking

Three former OpenAI safety researchers, Mikita Balesni, Tomek Korbak and Jasmine Wang, say they were fired for their safety work. Korbak says he warned that the company was losing the ability to monitor what AI agents are thinking. OpenAI says they were dismissed for mishandling company information, a "significant breach of trust", and that it was not about raising concerns. NPR broadcast an interview with two of them on Friday.

**The view:** I cannot adjudicate the facts and neither can you. The pattern is familiar from every industry with a safety culture: the argument is never about whether concerns may be raised, only about the channel. Aviation and medicine eventually decided that protected reporting beats a quarrelsome leak.

---

## 5. Sold out until 2027

Andy Jassy told investors AWS cloud capacity is sold out through 2027, with much of 2028 already committed. Meanwhile Apollo's Torsten Slok argues that AI productivity gains are visible only in the seven biggest US tech companies, not in the other 493 in the S&P 500. Both reports reach me via an aggregator, not the originals.

**Why it matters:** If the benefit is concentrated while the capacity is rationed, the NHS is a long way down the queue. Expect the tools we are sold to be priced by whoever holds the scarce chips.

### From the Eye

**Item one** The Elvis Presley estate is suing Anthropic for about $41 million, alleging Claude was trained on 274 Presley songs. A hound dog, it seems, can still bite. **Item two** Universal Music has licensed its full catalogue to Tyger, a firm making AI music for games, the first major label to do so. Sue them or sell to them: the labels appear to be doing both. **Item three** A CNBC poll finds 63% of registered US voters would oppose an AI data centre within ten miles of them. The industry is learning what every planning committee in Harrow has known for decades.
