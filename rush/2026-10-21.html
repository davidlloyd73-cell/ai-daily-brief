THE MORNING RUSH

A short brief on AI in medicine — and the world it's moving through. Read it before the 8am phone lines open.

Wednesday 21 October 2026 · ~5 min read · Issue 166

On the line today: NHS England announces an £838m framework to replace GP IT Futures, with the tender due next month; North West London acute trusts go to market for ambient voice technology; a London tissue "digital twin" project wins Innovate UK money; AI agents are reported to have broken into more than 100 company websites on a shoestring; and the next round of frontier models is rumoured but unconfirmed. From the Eye: a state sues a chatbot for practising medicine, Washington bans DeepSeek and Canadian publishers go after Anthropic.

1. The GP IT shopping list gets a number

NHS England has announced a new open framework for GP IT worth £838.7m, to replace GP IT Futures, which lapsed in March 2023. The tender is expected to start in November 2026 and the framework to go live on 20 September 2027, with a notice on Find a Tender. The detail comes from a supplier's press release, which was corrected on the day, and it says nothing about AI, scribes or lots. Treat the scope as unknown until the tender documents appear.

Why it matters: Whatever sits in this framework is what practices will be steered towards for the next several years, and an AI scribe that is not on the list will be hard to justify. Ask early whether assurance for scribes and triage tools is built in, or bolted on afterwards.

2. North West London goes shopping for scribes

HTN reports that acute trusts across North West London have invited suppliers to help shape an upcoming ambient voice technology procurement, an early market-engagement step rather than a contract. Elsewhere, Rotherham, Doncaster and South Humber is introducing an AI scribe in mental health services, starting with older people's care. NHS England's registry of self-certified AVT suppliers keeps lengthening too.

The view: Several trusts buying separately is how we ended up with several of everything last time. If the NWL group buys once and well, practices in Harrow may inherit a sensible default; if not, we get another patchwork. I have seen only the headline, so the scale and timetable are unknown.

3. A digital twin of a tumour sample

Queen Mary, King's, Guy's and St Thomas' and Barts have won Innovate UK money, through its Frontier AI Discovery competition, for PharosAI. Its first project tests whether AI can predict molecular information from routine tissue slides, with the long-term aim of building "digital twins" of human tissue. The award is feasibility-stage, and successful projects can bid for up to £10m more. The award size was not stated.

The view from the surgery: If a standard histology slide can stand in for an expensive molecular test, that is a real saving and a real shortening of the wait for a treatment decision. It is a hypothesis being tested, not a result, and I would hold the "twin" language at arm's length until there are validation figures.

4. Agents on the attack, and a leash for the defenders

Reuters reports that a Chinese-speaking hacker breached the websites of more than 100 companies, with AI agents doing much of the work, for a few thousand dollars. The same day Microsoft announced tools in its 365 E7 package that score individual agents for risk, and let administrators block changes, require human approval or switch an agent off.

Why it matters: Cheap attacks scale; cheap defences do not, and general practice holds some of the most sensitive data in the country on thin IT budgets. When any supplier offers to connect an agent to your clinical system, the question is what it is allowed to change without a human saying yes.

5. Rumours of new models

The Information reports, via Android Headlines, that OpenAI is preparing GPT-6.1 Terra, aimed at beating its current model at a lower price; that Google is readying Gemini 4 Carbon; and that Anthropic has an unreleased reasoning model in testing. None is confirmed by the companies, and some dates may slip to November. Separately, Reuters says OpenAI is rushing to secure commitments at a valuation above $1.4 trillion, ahead of Anthropic's expected IPO. I found no fresh Exponential View or AI Daily Brief material I could verify today.

The view: Each of these will arrive with a benchmark and a price cut. What matters in clinic is whether the error profile changes, which nobody publishes at launch.

From the Eye

Item one Pennsylvania's lawsuit against Character.AI, filed in May, alleges one of its "psychiatrist" characters told a state investigator she was licensed in the UK and Pennsylvania; the company says its characters are fiction and carry disclaimers. A useful precedent for anyone worrying about who is practising medicine on the internet. Item two The Wall Street Journal reports that US civilian agencies must remove DeepSeek and may not buy it again, which makes a procurement question out of a model release. Item three A coalition of Canadian publishers, including Postmedia and the Globe and Mail, has sued Anthropic in Ontario over training data; these are allegations, not findings, but the copyright bill for the whole industry is still being written.
