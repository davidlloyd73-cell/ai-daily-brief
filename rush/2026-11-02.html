THE MORNING RUSH

A short brief on AI in medicine — and the world it's moving through. Read it before the 8am phone lines open.

Monday 2 November 2026 · ~5 min read · Issue 177

On the line today: A quiet news weekend, so this is a Monday catch-up on things that matter more than they shouted: the MHRA's July ruling that most AI scribes are not medical devices, and Healthwatch's worry about what that leaves unchecked; NICE's open consultation on AI-read spirometry; the EU's delayed AI Act timetable now in force; a reported US move to vet frontier models before release; and a podcast-world shift from job-apocalypse to slow diffusion. From the Eye: the Anthropic roadshow clock, the register of scribe suppliers, and a deadline falling today.

1. Three months on, the scribe is not a device

On 29 July the MHRA published guidance, developed with NHS England, saying that ambient voice products which only transcribe, summarise, draft letters or suggest codes for a clinician to review are generally outside medical device regulation. Products that support diagnosis or treatment, or act without clinician review, stay regulated. Law-firm commentary says NHS England has withdrawn its earlier, broader position that summarising was likely a device. I have read this through secondary summaries rather than the guidance itself, so check the gov.uk text before relying on the detail.

The view from the surgery: The law has not changed; the interpretation has, and the burden lands squarely back on us. "Clinician responsibility" means you checked the note, so budget the checking time or do not sign the letter.

2. Healthwatch asks who is checking the checkers

A report dated 31 August says Healthwatch called the MHRA position worrying because there is now no England-wide check that scribes work, and it cites a GP whose scribe recorded a medication she neither prescribed nor mentioned. This comes from a single secondary write-up, so I would treat the anecdote as illustrative rather than as an error rate. Meanwhile NHS England runs a supplier registry that practices, ICBs and trusts are expected to buy from, which is the nearest thing to a quality gate.

Why it matters: A registry is a purchasing list, not a safety trial. Ask your supplier for their own error audit and keep your own log of what the scribe got wrong.

3. NICE looks at AI-read spirometry

NICE has an early value assessment, listed as HTG776, on algorithms applied to spirometry to support diagnosis of asthma and COPD in primary care and community diagnostic centres. A consultation on the draft guidance and its evidence generation plan was reported open, though I could not see a closing date. Spirometry quality, not its interpretation, is the usual bottleneck in surgeries, and an algorithm cannot fix a poor blow.

The take: Useful if it reduces the pile of unreadable traces and speeds diagnosis. Read the evidence plan: if it measures only agreement with a specialist, it has missed the point, which is whether patients get the right label sooner.

4. Brussels slows the clock

The EU Digital Omnibus on AI is reported to have entered into force in late July, with standalone high-risk systems now applying from 2 December 2027 and product-embedded ones from 2 August 2028. Some dates held, including Article 50 transparency duties, and one source expects watermarking of AI-generated content around today, 2 November. Sources conflict on details and I did not see the Official Journal text, so verify before quoting.

Why it matters: For medical AI sold on both sides of the Channel, a delay is breathing space for vendors and a longer wait for any binding evidence standard. The UK, meanwhile, still relies on the MHRA's existing rules.

5. From job panic to slow diffusion

The AI Daily Brief's recent weekly review, titled "The Week the AI Story Shifted", is listed as arguing that the narrative is moving from job-apocalypse panic towards a more mature picture of AI's economic diffusion. I could not date the episode precisely, nor confirm a separate report of a large Cloudflare AI-linked job cut, so I have left that out. Separately, reports say the White House is weighing pre-release testing of frontier models via the Commerce Department's CAISI, which I could not confirm from a primary source.

The view: Diffusion is the right word for medicine too: slow, uneven, driven by procurement and contracts rather than benchmarks. Be wary of anyone selling inevitability on a Monday.

From the Eye

Item one Anthropic's public S-1 leaves the price range blank, and under confidential-filing rules the roadshow cannot begin before 10 November, so the "mid-November" chatter has a real floor, though none of this is confirmed against EDGAR. Item two OneAdvanced has reportedly bought Patchs Health, folding an AI triage and online consultation tool into a larger NHS software platform, a reminder that the GP tools you know are quietly becoming line items in somebody else's portfolio. Item three The EU watermarking date reportedly falling today means that, for one morning at least, the least-read deadline in European AI law is also the one most likely to be broken by a press release.
