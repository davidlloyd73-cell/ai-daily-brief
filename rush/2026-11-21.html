1. An avatar explains the bronchoscopy

Chelsea and Westminster Hospital has launched what it calls the Lung Cancer Digital Avatar Project for patients on the suspected cancer pathway, HTN reported yesterday. Short videos, fronted by AI avatars standing in for members of the lung cancer team, explain the scans, the tests and what happens at each appointment. Written information and an AI question-and-answer function come in ten languages; the videos are English only for now. Trust clinicians built it with a firm called OcciAI and the Roy Castle Lung Cancer Foundation, after a charity-funded pilot in 2025. I found no published evaluation.

The view from the surgery: The fortnight after a two-week-wait referral is when patients ring us, frightened, with questions we half answer. Good information in Gujarati or Polish is worth having. I would want to know who checks what the question-and-answer function says about prognosis.

2. Epic opens a front door for other people's AI

Epic said on 18 November that authorised AI agents from other companies will be able to retrieve data from its record systems by the first quarter of 2027, using the Model Context Protocol, according to Becker's. It has not said which applications, or which categories of data. STAT reports that Epic's executives made the announcement at its HLTH event while answering, for the first time, a STAT investigation which found the company had selectively withheld data access from rival developers. Epic has over 40% of the American hospital record market and is the record system in several NHS trusts.

Why it matters: Yesterday's item was about record suppliers bundling the scribe. This is the other half: the supplier also decides which outside tools may read the record. Ask the same question of our GP system suppliers.

3. Wales cannot find enough scribes that pass

Digital Health and Care Wales has abandoned its ambient voice framework, worth an estimated £10 million before VAT, HTN reported on 4 November. The framework opened in August to build a pre-qualified list of suppliers. It was dropped after the first assessment stage because too few suppliers met the conditions of participation. The agency will consult the market and try again. I missed this at the time, and I have HTN's account only.

The view: England's registry lists 23 suppliers who certify themselves. Wales checked, and too few passed. One of the two countries has its threshold in the wrong place, and I would like to see the Welsh conditions before deciding which.

4. The worst day of the AI year

The Nasdaq fell 5.28% yesterday, wiping out its gain for 2026, and the S&P 500 and the Dow both closed in correction, CNBC and CNN report. Nvidia fell about 13% after Reuters said, citing two sources, that the SEC is examining its accounting; the shares lost 23% over the week, their worst since October 2008. Anthropic fell 8.5% to $88.91 on its second day, a few dollars above the $83 offer price. A recent AI Daily Brief episode was titled "Wall Street Has No Idea What Anthropic Is Worth"; I have the title only. The model writing this brief is Anthropic's.

The take: An inquiry is not a finding, and Nvidia's results this week were strong. But the NHS is signing multi-year contracts with firms whose prices assume the money keeps flowing, and that deserves a line in the risk register.

5. Four senators ask to see the plumbing

On Thursday, the day Anthropic began trading, Senators Mark Warner, Angela Alsobrooks, John Kennedy and Mike Rounds introduced the AUDIT AI Act. As reported, it would have the SEC write rules, within 180 days of enactment, requiring listed companies to disclose their AI infrastructure financing in quarterly and annual reports. Warner's worry is circular investment, where a chip maker funds the customers who buy its chips. Two Democrats, two Republicans, and so far only a bill.

Why it matters: Nobody outside can currently add up who owes what to whom in this boom. Friday showed what happens when the market guesses.

From the Eye

Item one East and North Hertfordshire Teaching NHS Trust has 1,000 Copilot licences from NHS England to use until March 2027, with the benefits evaluation to follow, per an HTN summary of its board papers on 19 November; I have one source, and note the order of events. Item two An HTN headline of 6 November says Heidi Health, the scribe supplier procured for 1,239 Midlands GP practices, is cutting its workforce by 18%; headline only, so I do not know which jobs, but practices might ask who is left on the support desk. Item three The American health department finalised a privacy rule on 16 November giving providers 15 days, down from 30, to hand patients their records, effective in January; nothing in it is about AI, but faster access is what feeds the patient-with-a-chatbot consultation.
