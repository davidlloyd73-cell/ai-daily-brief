## What Happens When AI Solves Your Life’s Work

_AI was supposed to upend whole industries, and so far it mostly hasn't — it's changed how people work, not whether fields exist. Then OpenAI dropped 372 novel mathematical proofs, solving 90 of the top 500 open problems at roughly three hours of compute each, and mathematics became the test case: what actually happens when AI solves a field's life's work?_

## The One Idea

### Mathematics may be AI's first true field-level disruption.

The industry-ending wave everyone predicted never quite arrived — coding is the most changed field and demand for coders keeps rising. But OpenAI's release of 372 novel mathematical results, including partial progress on the Riemann hypothesis and the Hodge conjecture, looks different in kind: a significant chunk of a field's hardest outstanding problems, solved in one drop, faster than humans can absorb. The questions now are which fields are next on the entropy ladder — and whether a 'math genie' negates mathematicians' life's work or unlocks things undreamed of.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Verification is the bottleneck, not generation

The mathematics story is a warning about checking, not just producing. Hundreds of machine-made proofs can outrun the human attention needed to confirm them. General practice faces the same shape with AI scribes: the note is produced in seconds, but a clinician must still read and sign it. Keep that review step protected and do not let volume erode it.

### Medicine sits well down the automation ladder

The edition cites a ranking in which medicine falls late because of incomplete information, with maths and coding first. That fits a surgery day: ambiguous histories, patient context and unwritten knowledge. It suggests tools that draft, summarise and sort will arrive long before anything that decides. It is one scientist's opinion, not evidence.

### Treat headline figures with caution

The gap between $68 billion and $50 billion for OpenAI's revenue came from loose reporting of a private company. Vendor claims about AI savings or accuracy deserve the same scepticism. Ask for audited or peer-reviewed numbers before a practice signs anything. No Zitron piece addressed this edition, so there is no opposing view today.

## NLW's Takes

### Watch two things: which field is next, and what mathematicians do with the genie

Either this disruption pattern spreads to other domains, or human and systemic inertia proves it's less inevitable than it looks. And the question for mathematics itself: does a mathematical genie negate all that hard work and learning — or let mathematicians do things undreamed of as of yet? Over the coming months, that's what we'll begin to see.
