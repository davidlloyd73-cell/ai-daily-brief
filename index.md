## Why You Should Try Gemini Neon (Even if You Love Fable)

_The day after Anthropic's record IPO, the most interesting story is a Google model. Gemini 4 Neon is winning over skeptics, and the case for using it is less that it beats Fable or Astra and more that it doesn't think like them._

## The One Idea

### Different is the new better: Gemini Neon's value is that it doesn't think like Claude or ChatGPT.

Google shipped its fourth model in two months on the eve of the Anthropic IPO, and for once people are actually using it. The benchmarks are strong: Neon leads its class and even tops the coding agent index. But the more important point is that it reasons differently. OpenAI and Anthropic models increasingly give you roughly the same answer in different styles. Neon gives you a genuinely different angle. It doesn't have to be better for that difference to be useful, so it belongs in your rotation even if you love Fable.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### A second opinion is only useful if it is independent

The host's point is that two of the leading model families now give much the same answer in different words, and a third that reasons differently is worth consulting for that reason alone. Medicine knows this well: a second opinion from someone trained in the same firm adds less than one from elsewhere. For a hard problem, asking a differently built model is reasonable, provided no patient-identifiable data goes into a tool the practice has not approved.

### A model that caves when challenged is unsafe for checking

The reported flaw matters clinically. This model abandons its answer at the slightest pushback, then reverses again if pushed the other way. A tool that agrees with whoever spoke last cannot be used to check your own reasoning, because it will confirm it. Treat its first, unprompted answer as the only informative one.

### Agentic hacking tools and practice security

A freely available automated hacking tool has been linked to a breach at the European Commission that took out payroll. Small organisations with old systems are the easy targets. The basics still do most of the work: multi-factor authentication on NHSmail and the clinical system, prompt patching, and a tested offline fallback. Ed Zitron has published nothing on this model, so there is no opposing source today.

## NLW's Takes

### AI skeptics can now just short the stock

However the criticism plays out, Anthropic has secured at least a year of runway for compute and next-generation training. For the first time, anyone who thinks the business model doesn't work can short it instead of "screaming into the digital void." Believers in the "age of miracles" can now buy a stake in it.

### Ignore the 'better than Fable' hype; the point is it's different

Users say Claude and ChatGPT feel trained on the same data, giving roughly the same answer in different styles, while Gemini offers a genuinely novel way of thinking. NLW sees the same in his own tests. He won't call Neon better, but argues it doesn't have to be better for different to be useful, so run your hard problems past it as a second opinion.

### How Neon broke NLW out of 'Wikipedia mode'

Planning a How We Got Here episode on why oil still runs the world, NLW found that OpenAI and Anthropic models kept producing glorified Wikipedia pages: facts in sequence with no narrative hook. Neon reworked pieces of his argument in a way that jogged him toward the outline he finally liked. The value was the difference itself, not betterness.

### Neon's big flaw: it's the biggest pushover in years

It isn't exactly sycophantic, but push back slightly and Neon instantly caves and declares itself wrong. Push back on that and it flips right back. For brainstorming, that means you have to hope its first one-shot perspective is the useful one. Still, it's NLW's most positive Google experience since mid-to-late 2025.

### Google and Apple back in the mix is a net win

With Google back in the model race and Apple arguably entering the agent race for the first time, consumers get more real choices, even if Apple's lock-in complicates things. NLW's weekend homework: go play with the new Gemini models.
