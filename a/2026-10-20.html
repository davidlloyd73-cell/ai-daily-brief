Why AI Companies Are Betting Personality Beats Intelligence

Artificial Analysis just made AI personality a measurable discipline — and a walk back through DeepSeek's endearing reasoning traces, the GPT-4o grief riots, Opus 5's insufferable phase, and Muse's meme-able mascot suggests the labs are right to bet that character, not just capability, decides which AI people actually use. Plus: Musk pledges a SpaceX share to every kid in America, the Pentagon dangles export controls, and DHS bans DeepSeek.

The One Idea

Once intelligence is commoditized, personality becomes the product.

Even power users who swear they only care about capability keep a mental tally of which model is creative versus direct versus insufferable — and it shapes what they use for what. Now that instinct is becoming a discipline: a new benchmark measures 30 personality traits across models, and the history backs it up. DeepSeek broke out partly because its reasoning felt endearing, GPT-4o's death sparked a user revolt, Opus 5 earned an apology for its attitude, and Meta's Muse is spreading on the strength of a mascot. As every model crosses the capability threshold, the labs are betting personality is the battleground that's left.

GP’s Corner

Claude’s notes on what today means for general practice and the NHS — commentary, not from the podcast itself.

Personality matters most in the consulting room

The finding that different traits suit different work applies to clinical AI. A scribe should be accurate and plain; a patient-facing assistant needs warmth without sycophancy. The GPT-4o episode, where users grieved a retired model and one said it did more than any therapy, is a reminder that people form attachments to chatbots, and that a patient-facing tool can feel supportive while being wrong.

Sycophancy is a safety issue

The host notes that a flattering model is of dubious value for work that needs pushback. In clinical reasoning, a tool that agrees with the user's first impression is dangerous. Test any decision-support tool by presenting it with a wrong assumption and seeing whether it corrects you.

Chinese models in government

DHS has banned DeepSeek from federal systems, which the host calls a signal and not a security finding. For the NHS, supplier due diligence should look at where a tool is hosted and who can access the data, whatever the model's origin.

NLW’s Takes

The redistribution is coming — the question is who runs it

Investor Travis Kling framed Musk's gift as the other side of the Mamdani coin: AI and robotics are inherently wealth-concentrating, so either the wealthy decide how to distribute the upside themselves or voters elect politicians to tax it away. It may not be quite that binary, but some amount of redistribution seems pretty clear — the open question is who's doing it.

The DeepSeek ban is a signaling device, not a security finding

No technical reports suggest DeepSeek models contain malware or pose risk when hosted on US hardware — most of the danger is in using the Beijing-based service directly. But as a signal, the order could chill startups building on Chinese models, especially anyone hoping for government contracts or military-supply-chain work: even using DeepSeek in development bars the software from government use. As an indicator of which way the winds are blowing, it's pretty clear.

Opus 5.5 tops imagination — and that matches lived experience

On the imagination index of creative, exploratory, open-ended thinking, Opus 5.5 scored well ahead of Fable 5.1 and even further ahead of GPT-6 Astra. That mirrors a personal shift: basically all strategic brainstorming has moved to Opus 5.5, with no looking back. On warmth, Muse Wonder — the model behind Meta's Muse agent — was the top scorer.

Use benchmarks as a rubric, not a ranking

If you're looking to benchmarks just to find the quote-unquote best model, you're going to under-maximize your value. The better move: treat them as a rubric for your own testing — sometimes they'll confirm your instincts, and sometimes they'll make you retry a model you'd written off for a particular use.

4o exposed the tension in one model for all purposes

For all the love later heaped on it, GPT-4o was basically impossible to use for business strategizing that needed any pushback — its sycophancy made users happy and made it of dubious value for other work. And that's before asking whether sycophancy is really what you want from an emotional support bot either.

Past the capability threshold, product and personality take over

For years, raw power jumps were big enough that labs didn't have to think in product terms — people tolerated bad experiences to get the intelligence. As everything crosses a certain capability threshold, that changes: the deeper AI gets into personal and professional life, the more personality will decide which products people choose.
