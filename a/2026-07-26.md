## The One Idea

### The skill now is managing agents, not chatting with a bot.

Ethan Mollick's updated guide draws a bright line between two interaction patterns: the old back-and-forth chat, where almost any model will do, and the new agentic workflow, where you delegate hours of work to a system that plans and acts on its own. That second pattern isn't just a new tool — it requires reprogramming how you think about AI. AIDB's free Summer Adventure is built to help you close your own "capability overhang": the gap between what AI can already do and what you're actually using it for.

## The Other Side

Zitron's answer to "you have a capability overhang" is that the overhang is a story told by people who need you to keep buying. In his account the tech industry has become a cargo cult, "people doing impressions of things that have worked before", and AI adoption is the purest example: executives who do not do the work mandate the tools because someone they admire said to. He is blunt about the demand side too, arguing the popularity of AI "has come entirely from social pressure and endlessly-discounted access", and that the moment real prices arrived customers started asking about ROI. He reads the industry's sudden enthusiasm for agentic loops as a revenue mechanism rather than a discovery, noting that Anthropic's Claude Code lead is allowed to burn upwards of a hundred and thirty thousand dollars a month in tokens, and saying flatly that it is unclear what benefits a loop might have.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Cargo Culture", 23 June 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/cargo-culture/

## The Truth Beneath

The variable both men circle is the gap between what AI can be shown doing and what it can be shown paying for. Both accept the demos are real: Zitron never disputes that a model checked Mollick's 195 references, he disputes that such anecdotes aggregate into economics. The empirical question that will resolve is whether skilled agentic use survives unsubsidised pricing, and the observable is renewal and seat-expansion on full-price agent tiers once free credits stop. The residue is pure worldview: whether slow uptake is a learning curve or a polite name for not worth the money. NLW is vindicated if heavy users keep paying real prices and firms show margin gains; Zitron is vindicated if usage falls away when the discounts do. Both are polemicists with skin in the game: NLW is launching a training programme premised on your overhang, Zitron sells subscriptions premised on certain collapse. Where they agree is the signal, and both say executives mandating AI they do not understand is a genuine failure mode. Watch full-price retention.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Draft, never send — the permissions lesson is the clinical one

The sharpest detail in this edition is that Mollick's two agents behaved differently on the same instruction: Claude drafted a reply, ChatGPT actually sent an email, because it had been granted send permission earlier and never asked again. Transpose that into a surgery and it stops being an anecdote. A scribe that drafts and waits is a wholly different clinical risk from an agent that files a referral, orders a test or messages a patient. The workable rule for general practice is the one Mollick arrives at the hard way: leave everything set to ask for approval until you know the system's failure modes, and make every output land in a queue with a named clinician accountable for it. If NLW is right that delegation is the real skill, the delegation still has to end at a human signature.

### The 195 references, and what would actually count as evidence

Thirty minutes of work, 195 references chased down, no invented page numbers — impressive, and precisely the sort of result that tells a practice manager nothing yet. The NHS question is not whether an agent can produce good work in a demonstration but what its error rate looks like under audit, on your patients, on a Monday morning. If Zitron is right that these stories are cargo cult in miniature, the discipline is cheap to adopt anyway: before buying anything agentic, decide in advance the number you would measure — letters returned for correction, coding errors, minutes per consultation — and measure it before and after. That one habit protects you whichever man turns out to be right.

### Close your own overhang, but be slow with the practice cheque book

There is a real asymmetry worth naming. For an individual GP the capability overhang is genuine and almost free to close: better prompting of the scribe you already have, dictating letters differently, using the tool on the correspondence that eats your evenings. For a practice or a PCN the same enthusiasm becomes a procurement decision with a contract attached, and that is where Zitron's warning bites hardest. Note too what Mollick did to make the agents useful — he connected them to Gmail and a Google Drive. Nothing patient-identifiable belongs in a general consumer connector, and any such link needs a DPIA and the usual information-governance route first, not afterwards.

## NLW's Takes

### Mollick is making agent-management feel approachable

NLW's take: rather than going model-by-model, Mollick draws a bright line between the old chat pattern and the new managing-agents pattern — deliberately making agent management feel accessible to people whose eyes glaze over at words like "harness." It's simple on purpose, because he's writing for a far less mature AI audience.

### Building your first app is the biggest unlock

NLW argues that vibe-coding your first application is one of the biggest unlocks available right now — more than almost anything else, it shifts you from seeing AI as an assistant that speeds up current work to something that unlocks entirely new capabilities. "Excursions" are projects that build something outlasting a single session.

### Everyone has a capability overhang

NLW's closing thesis: the 2026-defining idea is the capability overhang — the gap between what AI can do and what we're actually using it for. Almost no one is exempt, not even lab researchers or full-time AI content creators, because capabilities keep racing ahead faster than anyone can adopt them. The Summer Adventure is meant to help you close your own gap and have fun doing it.
