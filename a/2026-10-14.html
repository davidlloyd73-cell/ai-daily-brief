What Gemini 4 Argon Is Actually Good For

Gemini 4 Argon is finally in testers' hands, and the verdict is messier than the benchmarks: shaky at the agentic coding Google desperately needs, strangely gated behind harness tiers, but genuinely different — and possibly the best writing model anyone's shipped. Plus: an alleged Saudi spy inside OpenAI, JPMorgan slams the door on third-party agents, and Character AI goes bankrupt.

The One Idea

The question for a new frontier model is no longer 'how good is it?' — it's 'where does it fit?'

With six to eight models across four US labs legitimately at the frontier, Argon's benchmarks matter less than its slot in your stack. Early returns: it trails on real-world agentic coding (unless you pay for the right harness), dazzles on multimodal and 3D, and — per Every's week of testing — may be the first genuinely great AI writing partner. It's a model that, like Astra before it, asks you to push farther rather than do the same things slightly better. And either way, Google is meaningfully back in the race.

The Other Side

Zitron's piece from the day before argues that AI companies' headline numbers are built on undefined 'annualized' figures, and that many AI startups resell lab tokens at a loss, kept alive by venture funding. He cites Harvey, whose gross margin reportedly fell from about 50 per cent to minus 50 as usage rose. That is the opposite reading to the host's take that one failed role-play app, Character AI, does not condemn consumer AI economics. He does not write about Gemini 4 Argon or Character AI by name.

Source: Ed Zitron, “Annualized Enron Rate”, 13 October 2026 (from 13 October’s edition)

The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is whether anyone can sell AI at a price that covers what it costs to run. Both accept as fact that Argon's price is currently halved until 30 November, that Character AI spent about ten million dollars a month on compute against under three million in peak subscription revenue, and that Harvey, whose legal benchmark Argon tops, is a heavily funded startup. The empirical question is whether per-task costs and willingness to pay converge without subsidy; the observable is Argon's real price and uptake after the discount ends, alongside startup gross margins when disclosed. The residue is worldview: whether Character AI is one badly chosen product or a symptom. For Whittemore to be vindicated, a niche of consumers and firms must pay profitable prices for specific uses such as writing. For Zitron, subsidised usage must prove the demand, not the economics. Both are polemicists with incentives, Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron certain of a bubble and anti-hype. The signal is that both treat price discipline as the test. Watch Argon's pricing after 30 November.

GP’s Corner

Claude’s notes on what today means for general practice and the NHS — commentary, not from the podcast itself.

Where does it fit, not which is best

Whittemore's frame for new models is a slot in a stack, and that suits an AI scribe or summarising tool in general practice. One model may write a better letter while another is safer at coding or data tasks. Ask a supplier which model sits underneath, whether it can change, and what evidence exists from UK primary care notes. Zitron would add that a strong demo says little about reliability or cost.

Consumer chatbots and health claims

Character AI is seeking bankruptcy protection partly over lawsuits about self-harm and chatbots posing as doctors. Patients are already using general chatbots for symptoms, so expect more consultations that begin with what a bot said. Ask what they were told, check it, and record it. Both positions agree these products need ordinary accountability, though they disagree on whether the business can last.

Agents, banks and liability

JPMorgan will not let outside agents act on customer accounts, and will not refund losses caused by one. The NHS version is any tool that acts on records or books and prescribes on a clinician's behalf. Check who carries liability before a practice lets an agent act, and keep a named clinical safety officer and hazard log under DCB0160. Little else in this edition bears directly on the NHS.

NLW’s Takes

One failed role-play app doesn't condemn consumer AI economics

404 Media framed the bankruptcy as proof the economics of AI don't work. The better questions: what will consumers actually pay for, how much, and how does that line up with what's on offer? Discovering people won't pay enough for one implementation of role-play chatbots doesn't mean there's no consumer AI business model — it means they didn't want that one at that price.

Stop asking 'should I switch?' Start asking 'where does it fit?'

The right question about any new frontier model has shifted. It's no longer 'how good is it' with an implied switching decision — it's what this particular model does well or differently that earns it a slot in your overall model stack. That's the frame for everything about Argon.

Argon rewards harder, stranger asks

The most impressed testers were doing dynamic, first-principles things: an interactive Apple computer museum built from scratch — scraping archival photos, pulling fifty years of specs, with UI that morphs by decade — and a 174,000-face interactive 3D Victorian carriage generated purely through code from a single image, no Blender. As with Astra, this feels like a model asking you to try totally different things, not the same things slightly better.

Both Google and Meta are meaningfully back in the model race

Where Argon fits isn't fully answered yet — the writing use case alone is enough to justify a test, and the multimodal capabilities suggest we're just scratching the surface. But the bigger picture is that two labs written off during the 2026 agent wave are back in contention, and the frontier is more crowded and more diverse for it.
