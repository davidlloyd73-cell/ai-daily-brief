## The One Idea

### Personal agents are still an experiment — chief of staff, or proactive proxy employee?

The form factor has traction — Muse is growing, and we've been playing with this shape since OpenClaw exploded — but the open question is what these things are actually for. Are they the master coordinator of everything you do with AI, or a very specific proactive proxy-you that figures out how to be helpful without being asked? A few days into Claude Duet, NLW's answer: much less a shift in how he interacts with AI, much more an easy-to-set-up little baby version of himself that does the stuff he doesn't want to do. Valuable — but future-of-the-industry valuable remains to be seen.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Pushback that is based on something

The host's hardest model test is whether it disagrees for a reason. For clinical reasoning that is the right test: ask a tool to critique a differential, not confirm it. A tool that agrees with everything is a liability in medicine.

### Two models checking each other

For high-stakes research he has two different models grade each other's work. The same idea suits clinical documents: where an error would matter, a second independent check, whether a second model or a second clinician, catches mistakes a single pass misses. It does not replace the clinician.

### Personal agents and the starred-email problem

Duet's best use was drafting replies to emails he would otherwise return to days later. In practice, similar tools could help with routine administrative correspondence, but only after a data protection assessment, because they read the mailbox. Patient information should not go through a personal agent.

## NLW's Takes

### Treat personal agents as still experimental

Yes, there's early traction — especially with Muse — and the form factor has been around since OpenClaw exploded earlier this year. But the unresolved question: are these agents a chief of staff organizing everything you do, or a very specific proactive proxy-you that goes out and figures out how to be helpful without being asked?

### The hardest model test: pushback that's based on something

For a long time, models weren't much better than writing on paper because every idea was the best idea ever. For strategy work you don't want a yes-person — you want critical engagement grounded in actual reasoning, not a model trained that pushback is good. Dynamic, creative, adaptable, and confident is a really hard line to walk.

### Fable's real breakthrough was lowering 'completion energy'

It had been easy to start building things for a while — the activation energy was low — but projects hung at 80 or 90% complete. Fable brought the completion energy down to match, making the difference between starting things and actually getting them done.

### Claude has gotten so good at video editing you should experiment even if you don't need video

Especially with Opus 5.5, the models' video-editing jump is big enough to be worth exploring on its own. NLW's pipeline feeds Claude Code each day's transcript; it judges the most compelling self-contained 60-to-90 seconds, extracts the clip, builds visual assets around it, and packages it for upload.

### Proactive agents failed the juice-versus-squeeze test — until maybe now

The OpenClaw era experiments — individual claws on heartbeats and cron jobs, a chief-of-staff agent coordinating them, a persistent research agent monitoring AI adoption studies — all got shut down because they didn't add enough value to justify maintenance. Part of that is personal: 90% of NLW's calls are unscheduled and his inbox system is starring and clearing every couple of days. The new question is whether this generation lowers setup cost enough to flip the math.

### Explaining the flight to an agent takes as long as booking it

The canonical consumer pitches — flight booking, agentic shopping, restaurants — don't fit someone who books within one airline's miles ecosystem: explaining the variables and contingencies to an agent would take exactly as long as using the airline's own interface. The professional side of personal agents was always the only live question here.

### The ecosystem triage: all-in on two, just-enough on the rest

Third-party agents like Instinct and Town lose on the unwillingness to move work context out of ChatGPT and Claude; Muse skews too personal for now. Grokbot earns a 'stay just engaged enough to not start from zero' allocation for its X integration. Google isn't worth heavy investment yet — though NLW is optimistic that changes — and Microsoft is tracked professionally but skipped by a solo operator biased toward nimble tools.

### Don't sweat which personal-agent ecosystem you pick

The moment a feature works in one of these products, it becomes part of all of them within weeks if not days. If Dots' lack of proactivity is a common complaint, OpenAI will tweak it; if people prefer Duet's UI, the other labs will copy it.

### The verdict so far: a baby version of me, not a new way of working

Personal agents haven't become the main way NLW interacts with AI. They're an easy-to-set-up little baby version of himself doing a bunch of stuff he doesn't want to do — which is valuable, but whether it's future-of-the-industry valuable remains to be seen.
