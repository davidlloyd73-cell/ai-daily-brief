The AI Wealth Problem

An agentic swarm pulls off what looks like the first large-scale AI-powered cyber attack, Wall Street argues about whether the bubble is bursting, and Anthropic commits roughly 20% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund — reigniting a debate as old as the Gilded Age about who gets to decide what the 'public good' means when private AI wealth dwarfs public institutions.

The One Idea

AI wealth is going to be redistributed — the only question is by whom.

Anthropic committed roughly 20% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund and got a Gilded Age fight instead of applause. The same weekend, its draft safety bill angered safety advocates and accelerationists alike. Both controversies are proxies for the same two colliding problems: AI is concentrating extraordinary wealth in very few hands, and owning these models may simply be too much power for any one private institution. How that wealth and power interact with democracy is poised to be one of the biggest questions society faces in the coming years.

The Other Side

Zitron argues that the 2012 JOBS Act let companies stay private for so long that most of the growth is priced in before the public can buy, so IPOs mainly let early investors sell. He says Anthropic's reported two trillion dollar valuation rests on projected annual revenue of 200 billion dollars and a 30 trillion dollar market, and calls the AI boom "the largest valuation bubble in the history of technology". Only the opening part of the piece is free to read, so the proposed remedies are not covered here.

Source: Ed Zitron, “Premium: Venture Capital Is Killing Itself”, 16 October 2026 (from 16 October’s edition)

The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is who ends up owning, and being paid by, AI's upside. Both accept as fact that Anthropic is heading for a public listing at a valuation above a trillion dollars, that private markets set the price until now, and that a small group of founders and early investors hold much of the value. The empirical question is whether public investors earn a return after listing; the observable is how Anthropic and SpaceX shares trade against their issue prices over the following year. The residue is worldview: whether a company growing this fast should be judged on projected markets or on audited results. For Whittemore to be vindicated, wealth must be shared by choice, through funds or distributions, before governments force it. For Zitron, listings must prove to be exits for insiders and later buyers must lose money. Both are polemicists with incentives, Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron certain of a bubble and anti-hype. The signal is that both think current ownership is too concentrated. Watch how the stock behaves after the Anthropic listing.

GP’s Corner

Claude’s notes on what today means for general practice and the NHS — commentary, not from the podcast itself.

Pensions are the exposure that reaches the NHS

New York's pension custodian asking Anthropic to delay its listing, and reports of pension funds gaining from AI, are a reminder that AI valuations reach ordinary savers through pension funds. The NHS Pension Scheme is not invested this way, but many doctors' private pensions are, so a correction would matter personally, whichever of the two commentators is right.

Public benefit funds and health

Anthropic's fund may support labour-market and other public causes, and critics see a slush fund. Neither side has said anything specific about health. The relevant NHS point is only that large private philanthropy is not a substitute for public service funding.

Cyber attack on a coding tool

The OpenCode breach, with stolen keys used to run up bills, is a reminder to rotate and restrict API keys on any tool a practice or its suppliers use. Cheap agents are now attacking continuously, so multi-factor sign-in and prompt patching matter.

NLW’s Takes

This isn't the dot-com bust — it's a debt repricing

There are no massive overbuilds or canceled spending; spot GPU rental prices are up 17 days in a row. Mostly, bond investors are demanding more to finance risky data center projects, which is crimping neo-cloud valuations. NLW's advice: shy away from anyone leaning on weighty-but-inaccurate historical comparisons and look for the people getting into actual specifics.

The case for getting the labs public as fast as possible

NLW has a hard time believing people will accept Anthropic's super voting share plan, and expects much more resistance. But on the bigger question — incident risk dragging down public markets versus the visibility of a public company — there's a fairly strong argument that we want these companies public as soon as possible if we want more of a hand in determining their direction.

The wealth problem and the power problem are the same problem

The Public Benefit Fund amounts to a recognition that some AI wealth will have to be redistributed — either voluntarily, or by someone else doing the redistributing, and Anthropic understandably wants to do it itself. But that runs headlong into the concern that owning these models already concentrates too much power in one private institution. The discourse around the fund and the safety bill are both proxies for how AI wealth and power will interact with democracy.
