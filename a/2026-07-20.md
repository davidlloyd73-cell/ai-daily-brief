## How to Get the Most Out of Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol

_No new-model drama today (that's Kimi's job) — instead a practical field guide to squeezing every drop out of Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, from managing a more tenacious model's boundaries to raising your own ambition until the limits reveal themselves._

## The One Idea

### Every big model leap demands you unlearn your old prompts — and raise your ambition.

With Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol in hand, the tips converging across the discourse point two directions. First: the way you prompted last generation now often hurts you — stale rule lists, brevity hacks, and maxed-out settings all backfire on more tenacious, more capable models. Second and more important: the real unlock isn't better prompting, it's higher ambition — pushing models onto high-leverage impact work, inviting them in as co-creators of the process, and adopting new interaction patterns like loops. The individual lesson has an organizational twin: stop using AI to do the same work faster and start unlocking categories of work that weren't possible before.

## The Other Side

Zitron argues the 'new interaction patterns' this edition celebrates - above all loops - are not a productivity breakthrough but a desperate industry ploy to get users to burn more tokens for revenue. He notes that within a day of each other Jensen Huang and Claude Code's Boris Cherny intimated that 'the age of prompting is over' in favour of loops, and observes dryly: 'It's unclear what benefits a loop might have.' To him the whole 'raise your ambition, adopt new rituals' framing is cargo-culting: a tech industry out of ideas, mistaking activity that resembles success for the real thing, with no evidence of return. The unlock, he would say, is imaginary; the token bill is not.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Cargo Culture", 23 June 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/cargo-culture/

## The Truth Beneath

The single variable both men orbit is whether these new interaction patterns - loops, agentic delegation, 'raise your ambition' - actually convert extra tokens and effort into measurable output, or merely ritualise spend. Both accept the same facts: the models are more capable, and loops burn far more compute. The empirical question that will resolve is whether organisations adopting these patterns post real, attributable productivity or margin gains; the observable that settles it is audited output or controlled-study data, not testimonials from vendors' own staff. The residue is pure worldview: NLW reads the new patterns as capability waiting to be unlocked, Zitron reads them as a cargo cult burning tokens to feed a bubble. NLW is vindicated if ambitious adopters show durable, measured gains; Zitron if the spend climbs while output does not. Both are polemicists with incentives - NLW bullish and anti-doom for an audience of buyers, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype for subscribers who reward it - so the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: loops cost more, and nobody yet has clean outcome data. Watch whether any independent study ties loop-based workflows to measured productivity.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Set boundaries the way you would with a locum you don't yet trust

The most portable lesson for the surgery needs no view on the hype. As models get more tenacious, the edition's advice is to state the few hard boundaries explicitly - 'prepare the message as a draft, don't send it,' 'use only the sources I've given you,' 'leave approved dates unchanged.' For anyone using an AI scribe or letter-drafting tool this is the safety rail that matters: the more capable the tool, the bigger the real-world consequence of it acting unbidden - a letter sent to a patient, a coding change, an unchecked source. Write the boundary down once, clearly, and check the tool honours it before you rely on it.

### Automate the admin, but measure before you scale - the loops question cuts both ways

The edition urges you to move AI onto higher-leverage work and to adopt 'loops' where an agent keeps working until a target is met. If NLW is right, loop-based agents could one day take genuinely tedious practice admin - recalls, QOF chasing, first-draft referral letters - end to end. If Zitron is right, loops mainly burn tokens and subscription money for motion that resembles progress without measured benefit. The disciplined path for a practice is the same whoever wins: pick one workflow, record a baseline (time taken, errors, safety events), and only expand what shows a real reduction in workload or a genuine safety gain. Adopt because it demonstrably helps, not because everyone else is.

### Resist the pull to 'raise ambition' into clinical decisions

The One Idea is to attempt bigger, harder things until the model's real limits show. That is fine for drafting and admin, but in clinical work the limits are exactly where patients get harmed, and 'find the edge by pushing' is the wrong method for diagnosis or prescribing. Keep the ambition aimed at the paperwork and the thinking-aloud - a sparring partner for a knotty referral, a blind-spot pass on a service redesign - and keep a clinician firmly between the model and any decision that touches a patient.

## NLW's Takes

### Recorded before the Kimi panic

NLW flags that this episode was taped Thursday afternoon as markets freaked out over Kimi 'So' tearing value off the Nasdaq — so if Dario and Sam panic-release new Fable and GPT versions, that's why they aren't covered here.

### The rambler shall inherit the earth

NLW champions voice dictation — ChatGPT's native dictation is best-in-class even without Whisperflow. In a world where AI needs more context, rambling a stream of consciousness often feeds the model better than a hyper-precise typed note that leaves context out.

### Dialing everything to max is emotionally hard to resist

NLW notes the temptation to crank settings to max on every problem — wouldn't you always want the most intelligence? But it's increasingly clear that's not optimal even before costs, which is why OpenAI is giving discrete guidance to do less.

### Assume no limits — then find where they actually are

NLW's core takeaway: every big intelligence jump requires the hard work of re-testing old habits that no longer help, and finding new techniques — sometimes whole new interaction patterns like loops — that unlock the new capability. The through-line is to ratchet up ambition and attempt the biggest, hardest things so the real limits reveal themselves.

### The organizational version of the same lesson

It's easy for businesses to default to using AI for the work they already do — just faster, cheaper, or slightly better. The real unlock is a new relationship with work and entirely new categories of work that weren't possible before. Harder to figure out, but far more exciting.
