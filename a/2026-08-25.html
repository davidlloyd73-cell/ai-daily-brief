What the Top AI Users Are Doing Differently

OpenAI's own data says the gap between the most advanced AI users and everyone else has ballooned from 2.6X in January to 8.3X by June — and the whole difference is agents. Today's episode digs into what frontier firms are actually doing differently, plus Meta's imminent consumer agent, NVIDIA as the central bank of compute, and a peek at Microsoft employees' AI bills.

▶ Listen — today’s brief (4 min)

The One Idea

Agentic use compounds: the top AI users are pulling away from everyone else.

In January, the most advanced AI users were using 2.6 times as much AI as average users. By the end of June, per OpenAI's own research, that gap had grown to over eight times. The reason is agents: frontier firms have moved from chat-based assistance to delegation — giving agents context, tools, plugins, and skills to do systems-level work — and because agents take on increasingly valuable work, their lead compounds. The average users just haven't made the jump, and the gap is poised to keep growing.

The Other Side

Zitron attacks the same OpenAI research this edition is built on, and goes after its central instrument: the token. He argues token counts measure subsidy rather than work, since customers still "burn anywhere from $20 to $40 in tokens for every dollar of software spend", and he notes that when OpenAI and Anthropic moved larger companies onto token-based billing this year, "enterprises almost immediately started cutting token budgets" because "nobody could actually point to anything improving". He then turns OpenAI's own economists against the headline: buried on page 35 of their 69-page report is the finding that "revenue per employee is not meaningfully associated with output tokens per employee" — so a firm burning eight times the tokens is not demonstrably eight times more productive. On agents he doubts the delegation story itself, describing the typical rig as "a contrived series of harnesses, prompts, API calls and burned tokens" that "requires constant maintenance to do some stuff sometimes".

Source: Ed Zitron, “The AI Hater's Manifesto”, 25 August 2026

The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is whether output tokens are a valid proxy for work done. Both accept the same facts: frontier-firm token use really has grown roughly seventeen-fold, agentic traffic really is most of enterprise output, and adoption really is lopsided between firms. The empirical question that will resolve is whether the token gap ever shows up in outputs — the specific observable is revenue, margin or headcount per employee for the heavy-agentic cohort against their sector peers over the next few years, which is precisely the correlation OpenAI's own economists could not find. The residue is pure worldview: Whittemore reads high token burn as capability being exercised, Zitron reads it as an inefficient product being subsidised. Whittemore is vindicated if the 8.3X cohort visibly outperforms on ordinary business measures once inference is no longer sold below cost; Zitron if usage stays high while performance stays flat and budgets are cut at renewal. Both are polemicists with incentives — Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — and the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: neither disputes that adoption is now real, broad and very unevenly distributed. Watch for anyone publishing an outcome measure, rather than a usage measure, for the frontier cohort.

GP’s Corner

Claude’s notes on what today means for general practice and the NHS — commentary, not from the podcast itself.

Usage is not outcome — an old lesson in new clothes

The whole episode rests on tokens as a proxy for work, and the counter-argument is that nobody has shown the tokens turn into anything. General practice has been here before: appointment counts, QOF points and referral rates all measured activity rather than benefit. If a practice or an ICB starts quoting AI usage figures, the sensible question is the one both sides here failed to settle — what changed for the patient, or for the clinician's day, that we can actually measure?

Where the ladder is safe to climb in a surgery

The 'use case ladder' — generation, synthesis, execution, maintenance — is a useful frame for a GP setting, and it also marks where the risk starts. Generation and synthesis are what an AI scribe already does: draft the consultation note, pull together a discharge summary. Execution and maintenance mean software acting inside the clinical record, and that is a different governance question entirely. If Zitron is right that agentic rigs need constant babysitting, the failure mode in a surgery is not lost time but a wrong entry in a live record; if the Brief is right, the gain will come from the boring end — recalls, coding, documentation — not from anything clinical.

The legal 108X figure, read from a Medical Examiner's chair

The fastest growth in agentic use is in legal work: comparing terms, flagging deviations, drafting, monitoring — with humans keeping the risk judgement and the accountability. That division of labour is precisely the one that would apply to medical examiner scrutiny or significant event work: software can read every note and flag what departs from the expected pattern, but the judgement of whether it mattered, and the signature under it, stays human. Worth watching, because the pattern arrives in medicine through the coroner's and governance side long before it arrives at the consulting room door.

NLW’s Takes

OpenAI's Sol price cut isn't about Anthropic — it's about the model stack

GPT-56 Sol drops to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, from $5 and $30, following cuts to Luna and Terra last month. Many read it as pressure on Anthropic ahead of its IPO, but the more likely driver: business customers won't just use the most expensive state-of-the-art model for everything anymore, and to the extent OpenAI has the compute to deliver frontier models cheaper, it makes sense to do so.

Before you scream 'circular deals' — NVIDIA is tapped out at home

NVIDIA doesn't operate its own fabs, so growing chip revenue is limited by supplier constraints it can't control. Turning investment outward — $42.3 billion in private companies as of March, with a higher number certain this week — supports the entire AI economy and, in turn, keeps NVIDIA's revenues strong for years to come.

The jobs apocalypse narrative is finally losing its grip

One of the best shifts in AI narratives right now is moving away from the accepted-without-question premise that AI is obviously job-destroying. Certain roles will genuinely be obviated and society should be ready to support the people affected — but the idea of a radical, rapid jobs apocalypse was never accurate.

Who needs a pause AI movement when you've got corporations?

Institutional inertia is doing the work AI doomers wished regulation would: even with incredible technology, society and the economy adapt slowly, and that slowness is the real governor on the pace of transformation.

It's not secret satisfaction — it's mind muscle memory

We all get stuck in the patterns of how we've always done things. Building a new, less comfortable mind muscle feels exhausting when we know exactly how long the old way takes — which is precisely why studying the people who have broken out of that muscle memory matters so much.

OpenAI buried its best enterprise research

OpenAI's 'Enterprise Signals: What Frontier Firms Are Doing Differently' puts hard numbers around how leading firms use AI — and the company barely promoted it. It only surfaced widely when A16Z reposted it in their charts of the week. Great stuff; please promote it more heavily next time.

The unlock wasn't the models — it was the patterns

It's great that OpenAI is making models and harnesses work better for knowledge work, but that's not why agentic use has grown among non-engineering knowledge workers. It's grown because we've started figuring out the patterns that actually allow agents to thrive in our own contexts.

The next leap is multiplayer AI, not individual AI

Even frontier users climbing into execution and maintenance are mostly running agents in individual silos. The next generation of gains will come at the intersection of different teams — team AI rather than just individual AI. And with frontier firms racing ahead, the numbers should be a wake-up call for anyone not deploying agents at scale yet.
