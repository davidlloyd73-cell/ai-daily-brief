## What the Best Business AI Users Are Doing Different

_KPMG's Q3 pulse of 2,100 senior leaders splits the enterprise world into two camps — companies still experimenting with AI and companies already banking ROI — and the gap between them reads like a roadmap: harness layers, model routers, sovereignty strategies, and a management layer that treats AI economics as seriously as AI capability. Plus Meta's best month since 2022, Anthropic's pre-Thanksgiving IPO sprint, and TPUs in orbit._

## The One Idea

### The best business AI users aren't buying better models — they're building better management layers.

KPMG's Q3 pulse survey of 2,100 senior leaders across 20 countries breaks organizations out by where they sit on the AI journey, and the distance between the experimenters and the established-ROI companies maps where everyone else is headed: 86% of mature organizations run a formal AI harness layer, more than half have enterprise-wide sovereignty strategies and C-suite accountability for AI-informed decisions, and nearly a quarter have built model routers. Use cases are shifting from pure efficiency toward revenue, cost control is maturing into economic management — and despite all the new discipline, planned spend is still going up.

## The Other Side

Zitron's argument is that the AI boom's revenue is mostly the labs and hyperscalers paying each other. He estimates that OpenAI and Anthropic are 70 to 80 per cent of AI compute demand, that 80 per cent of their enterprise revenue comes from 1 per cent of customers, and that those customers are largely venture-funded startups reselling tokens at a loss.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Dead Money", 29 September 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/dead-money/

## The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is whether Anthropic's revenue is durable and broad. They accept the same facts: huge losses in 2025, very rapid growth since, and a heavy reliance on a few big customers. The empirical question is whether growth comes from many paying businesses with real return on investment or from venture-funded intermediaries, and the observable that settles it is the audited customer breadth, margin and retention in the IPO filing, plus whether enterprise token spend stops falling. The worldview residue is whether a ten-times growth rate extrapolates. For Whittemore to be right, the numbers must show diversified, sticky revenue. For Zitron to be right, concentration and subsidised usage must dominate and funding conditions tighten. Both are polemicists with incentives: Whittemore is bullish and anti-doom, Zitron is bubble-certain and anti-hype. The signal is where they agree despite opposite priors, that the filing, not the commentary, will tell us. Watch the customer concentration disclosures.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### If Whittemore is right: the management layer is the value

KPMG's finding that mature organisations run a formal harness layer, with accountable executives, is the kind of governance a practice or PCN could copy: a named owner, an approved-tool list, and a log of what the AI was used for.

### If Zitron is right: price and availability are not guaranteed

If AI labs depend on venture funding and subsidised usage, today's cheap scribe could cost more later. Build a business case that survives a price rise, and keep a manual fallback.

### OpenAI's safety-team firings

Whatever the merits, it is a reminder that internal safety processes are not independent assurance. For clinical tools, look for independent evaluation, not the supplier's own.

## NLW's Takes

### Judging Anthropic on 2025 numbers is a pre-AI reflex

The leaked S1 shows an $8 billion operating loss on $4.6 billion of 2025 revenue, and the doomsaying followed. NLW's strongly held position: with more than 10x growth in 2026, investors won't price this deal on last year's numbers — if the IPO underperforms, the 2025 operating loss won't be the reason.

### Whatever you think of the firings, formal channels can't come fast enough

From the outside it's impossible to judge whether OpenAI was justified, because we don't know what was leaked — and interpretations will mostly track what people already believe about AI safety. The point everyone should agree on: this is exactly why formal channels for reporting and third-party evaluation need to be up and running as soon as possible.

### Enterprise adoption isn't just an enterprise story

Even solopreneurs and freelancers should care where big companies are on the adoption curve: many interact with enterprises as service providers, and enterprise behavior shapes how AI itself evolves. If Wall Street decides volume-up-spend-down is the permanent trend, it changes how much investors are willing to backstop the infrastructure build-out.

### Token budgets too early can strangle the experiment

One of NLW's standing soapboxes: being overly restrictive with usage or token budgets during the experimentation phase can be fairly limiting — though it's hard to judge individual programs without seeing what organizations actually count as a budget.

### Cost discipline isn't shrinking the spend

For anyone worried that the new focus on cost efficiency means decreased investment: KPMG found average planned AI investment over the next 12 months jumped from $186 million in Q1 to $210 million in Q3. NLW's view — we're still barely scratching the surface of what we will ultimately spend on intelligence.
