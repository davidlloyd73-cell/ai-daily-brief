## The Most Concerning AI Bubble Signals

_The bubble callers are back — Gartner's neocloud doomsday, GPUs gathering dust in Amazon warehouses, data center debt landing on insurer balance sheets, and revenue concentrated in a sliver of VC-backed power users. Today's episode sorts the signals that actually matter from the proxy fights, and for each one, names exactly what would have to happen to turn caution into alarm._

## The One Idea

### Every bubble signal is a proxy fight for one question: demand.

Warehoused GPUs, distressed neocloud bonds, pension fund exposure, frozen IPOs — each has its own logic, but they all collapse into whether AI demand ultimately exceeds what's being spent on the infrastructure. Most of today's warning signs aren't yet what the doomsayers claim: the GPU pile is a power problem, not a demand problem; data center debt isn't being disguised the way subprime was; the neoclouds are minnows the industry keeps backstopping. But each has a clear escalation path — and the Anthropic IPO is about to replace speculation with audited quarterly answers.

## The Other Side

Zitron argues the AI boom needs revenue, debt and venture funding all to keep growing at once. He says nine AI-linked stocks make up about 46 per cent of the Nasdaq 100, that Amazon has billions of GPUs waiting in warehouses, and that around 80 per cent of the top labs' enterprise revenue comes from loss-making startups. He cites Gartner's prediction that most neoclouds will cease to exist as independent firms by 2030, and reads the stored chips as evidence that supply exceeds demand.

Source: Ed Zitron, "AI Had Better Work Out", 20 October 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-had-better-work-out/

## The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is demand: whether customers will pay enough for AI to cover the infrastructure bill. Both accept as fact that CoreWeave's debt has traded at distressed levels, that Amazon has staged billions of dollars of chips awaiting power, that the labs lean on a few large customers, and that Gartner has predicted widespread neocloud failure. The empirical question is why the chips sit idle; the observable is whether GPU rental prices and neocloud financing hold up over the next two quarters, and what Anthropic's audited results show. The residue is worldview: whether concentration is a bubble signature or an early stage of a real platform shift. For Whittemore to be vindicated, idle chips must prove a power problem and enterprise demand must broaden. For Zitron, rental prices must fall and a neocloud must fail without rescue. Both are polemicists with incentives, Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron certain of a bubble and anti-hype. The signal is that both identify revenue concentration as the key risk. Watch the first audited quarterly numbers.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Stock-market bubble versus public funding

Whether the bubble bursts is a question for investors, not for the NHS, but a 20 to 30 per cent correction would reduce the value of private pensions many clinicians hold. Public spending on digital tools is unaffected directly. A credit crisis would be a different matter, and both commentators agree that is the more dangerous path.

### Supplier risk behind AI scribes

Both positions agree that revenue is concentrated in a few labs and that many AI companies are venture-funded. A scribe or triage product built on such a start-up carries the risk that prices rise or the firm folds. Ask for data export, exit terms and a named fallback in any contract.

### Data centres in the UK

Microsoft cancelled an Essex data centre over grid delays, which is relevant to anyone hoping for UK-hosted AI services. UK data residency may stay scarce and costly for some time.

## NLW's Takes

### Everything else is a proxy fight for demand

Every talking head in this debate has a dog in the fight, but there's one through line that ultimately matters: how much AI demand will there be, and is the value of that demand higher than what's being spent on the infrastructure? Every other bubble signal is a proxy for that question.

### Three neocloud signals that would actually be alarming

One: a neocloud gets into trouble and nobody rides to the rescue. Two: distress spreads from CoreWeave's own bonds to project debt for data centers leased to Meta, Microsoft, or Google — markets doubting the tenant, not just the landlord. Three: a hyperscaler or lab walks away from capacity because of demand, not power — Microsoft's UK exit was blamed specifically on power delays, and a demand-blamed cancellation would be a totally different story.

### Warehoused GPUs are a power problem, not a demand problem

Critics cast the AWS story as NVIDIA channel-stuffing and proof that demand is soft — but Amazon wouldn't order tens of billions in chips to warehouse them indefinitely, GPUs still trade at a premium with old hardware selling above original price, and the Occam's razor read is fear of losing future allocation. The dark fiber comparison fails: that was too little demand for installed infrastructure; this is too little infrastructure to service demand.

### The subprime comparison misses what made subprime toxic

The problem in 2008 wasn't chopping mortgages into CLOs — it was rating them investment grade and treating them as safe as Treasuries, with the structuring explicitly used to obfuscate risk. Data center debt today sits on balance sheets as ordinary corporate bonds, sometimes investment grade and sometimes not, and no one is pretending it's safe as houses. What would change the picture: disclosed exposure jumping by orders of magnitude into a handful of PE-owned insurers, or debt repackaged into securities rated higher than the loans underneath.

### On too-big-to-fail, that ship has already sailed

Anthropic's and Musk's public equity gifts revived fears that the labs are engineering bailout protection — but the government didn't stand aside for GM in 2008, and it's massively unlikely to let a trillion-dollar company blow a hole in the economy regardless of who holds the shares. The 'engineering a bailout ahead of a crash' theory doesn't hold up either; the escalation signal to watch is AI companies asking for explicit federal guarantees, loan programs, or financing backstops before any crisis.

### The Anthropic IPO is the test that resolves this

Frothy markets often top on a catalyst that breaks the suspension of disbelief, and for many the Anthropic IPO fits the bill. Pricing at $1.5 trillion after months of $2 trillion talk would be not great but not alarming; pricing at $1.5 trillion and then getting a weak market reception would confirm a lot of people's worst fears about the AI trade.

### Bubble calls have been wrong every year — carry salt anyway

The bubble-burst narrative recurs around this time every year: 2024's scaling-wall panic preceded the first reasoning models, and last year's 'enterprises won't keep paying' fears — embodied in the MIT 95%-of-pilots-fail report — preceded enterprise spend booming at around 50% a quarter. Previous calls being wrong doesn't make future ones wrong too; it just means every bombastic headline deserves a bag of salt while we wait for the Anthropic IPO to deliver a more definitive answer.
