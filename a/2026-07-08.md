## AI Costs Are Surging and the Cheap Model Fix Might Not Last

_A summer that was supposed to be slow is instead drowning in model announcements — GPT 5.6, Grok 4.5, Meta's Muse, MiniMax M3 Pro. But the real story sits underneath the noise: with AI costs surging in the agentic era, the go-to fix has been switching to cheap Chinese open-weight models. A Reuters report says Beijing may be about to slam that door shut._

## The One Idea

### If China stops open-sourcing frontier models, the cheap-model fix for surging AI costs may not last.

The dominant answer to the emerging token-cost crisis has been blunt: cap spending, or switch to a cheaper Chinese open-weight model. A Reuters report that Beijing is exploring blocking overseas distribution of its leading models threatens the second option. That wouldn't solve the underlying cost problem — it would just force it back onto Western alternatives: NVIDIA's Nemotron, Google's Gemma, Microsoft's Frontier Tuning, Thinking Machines' Tinker, and model routers that pick models on capability and risk alike. Either way, the enterprise AI buyer's life gets more complicated, not less.

## The Other Side

Zitron thinks the China panic driving this edition is, in his words, "living in a child's fantasy": the feared thing — cheap Chinese open models matching premium American ones — has already happened, and nothing broke. His deeper objection is that the cheap-model fix was never a fix, because nobody sells AI at a profit; subscriptions amount to "selling $40 for a dollar", so switching models just changes who absorbs the subsidy. He reads the surging-cost story as the bill finally arriving: firms that were forced to pay the actual cost of tokens are now pulling back, which to him reveals demand as manufactured rather than organic. Whether Beijing closes the door is beside his point — an industry whose only answer to its costs is a cheaper loss-making model has no business model, and should be allowed to fail rather than protected.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Let AI Burn", 7 July 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/let-ai-burn/

## The Truth Beneath

The single variable both men orbit is whether the true, unsubsidised cost of agentic AI can fall to a price businesses will actually pay. Both accept two facts: token costs in the agentic era are surging, with buyers capping spend or trading down; and Chinese open-weight models currently deliver near-frontier performance far cheaper. The empirical question that will resolve is whether Beijing actually restricts overseas distribution and, if it does, whether Western alternatives — Nemotron, Gemma, frontier tuning, fine-tuned specialists, routers — hold per-task costs down; the observable that settles it is enterprise per-task inference pricing over the next two quarters. The pure-worldview residue is whether cheapness reflects real efficiency gains or subsidy that must end. NLW is vindicated if costs keep falling through Western routes even without Chinese weights; Zitron if their withdrawal exposes unpayable true costs and spending contracts. Both are polemicists with incentives — NLW bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — so the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: frontier AI at list price is unaffordable for scaled agentic use. Watch whether Beijing formalises the restriction, and what happens to per-task prices.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Your scribe is priced per seat; your vendor pays per token

AI scribes in UK practice sit on top of exactly the model economics this edition describes. If the cheap-model route narrows and per-token costs rise, vendors protect margin by raising prices or quietly swapping in cheaper models underneath the product. Two questions worth putting to any scribe supplier: which models sit under the tool, and does a model change trigger re-validation and notification? If NLW is right, routers will soon pick a different model per task — which makes 'which model wrote this letter?' a live clinical-governance question. If Zitron is right, budget on the assumption that today's scribe pricing is subsidised and will rise.

### Don't build clinical workflow on geopolitically contingent models

Chinese open-weight models were never a serious option for NHS patient data, so Beijing's possible export block matters less directly to primary care than to enterprise buyers. But the general lesson lands: any clinical workflow that depends on a specific model's continued availability — for price, capability or interpretive style — carries a supply risk that procurement rarely prices in. Favour tools whose vendors can demonstrate they can swap engines without degrading output, and keep the paper-and-dictation fallback workflow alive.

### Zitron's stress test is good practice management

Whichever man is right, his discipline is useful: could the practice function next month if the scribe trebled in price or the vendor folded? Adopt AI for the time it genuinely saves — but keep the staff skills and the fallback process warm. That costs little if NLW is right, and a great deal of pain avoided if Zitron is.

## NLW's Takes

### The best models aren't the ones we're seeing

Because testers say they've had GPT 5.6 for months — implying it finished training before Mythos and Fable 5 were even revealed — the implication is that the labs are not shipping their most state-of-the-art models. What's public trails what's internal.

### Blocking Chinese models doesn't fix the cost problem

NLW argues the token-cost crisis of the agentic era has nothing to do with Chinese open weights — it's about the cost of provisioning the frontier and compute shortages. The two blunt fixes so far are spending caps (Tesla just imposed one company-wide) and switching to cheaper models; taking the second off the table just relocates the pressure.

### The enterprise AI buyer's life is getting harder

NLW's bottom line: these trend lines are already set — the need for cheaper models and smarter architectures exists whether or not Chinese models plug in. Even the growing possibility of China cutting off the frontier will create big market openings for Western model approaches, but it means AI buyers face more complexity, not less.
