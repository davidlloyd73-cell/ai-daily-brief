## The Big Ways AI Just Changed

_June is over, and NLW makes the case that it was one of the most consequential months in post-ChatGPT history — a matched pair with May that took AI from the subsidy era into token scarcity, then detonated with Fable 5, a government export-control shutdown, and the first real open-weight DeepSeek moment for the West._

## The One Idea

### June was the month AI's second great transition became real — cost, sovereignty, and government all at once.

May shifted AI from the subsidy era to token scarcity. June made it real: Walmart and Uber capped token budgets, Anthropic's Fable 5 stunned everyone, then the US government used export controls to shut it down — and suddenly companies had both a cost and a sovereignty reason to diversify away from closed frontier models. Open weights, routers, custom post-trained models, and harness engineering all moved from curiosity to boardroom question. The rest of 2026, NLW argues, is about figuring out how to actually operationalize this new capability.

## The Other Side

Where NLW reads June's token budgets, Fable 5, and the scramble toward open weights as AI maturing into a real, cost-disciplined industry, Zitron reads the same month as the bubble's unit economics finally showing through. Seizing on the Bank for International Settlements' annual report, he notes the five largest hyperscalers are set to spend over a trillion dollars on AI capex across 2025-26, commitments that are "outpacing earnings and free cash flow" and forcing firms to issue debt. His warning is that disappointing returns could turn the capex boom into what the BIS calls a "protracted investment bust", cascading through indebted suppliers like Oracle and CoreWeave. To him, cheaper tokens and efficiency drives are not maturation but a sector discovering it cannot afford its own product.

Source: Ed Zitron, "The AI Industry Is Losing", 30 June 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-ai-industry-is-losing/

## The Truth Beneath

The single variable both men orbit is whether the enormous money flowing into AI compute converts into durable, defensible economic returns. Both accept two facts: hyperscaler capex is vast and currently outruns free cash flow, and providers are tightening per-token pricing after the subsidy phase. The empirical question that will resolve it is whether enterprises keep expanding AI spend at genuine positive return, or whether the big four guide capex down for lack of it; the observable that settles it is Q3-Q4 2026 hyperscaler capex guidance set against operating cash flow, plus any audited inference margin. What remains pure worldview is interpretation: Whittemore reads discipline and diversification as an industry maturing, Zitron reads the identical signals as the economics finally biting. NLW is vindicated if token efficiency and open weights let firms scale AI profitably; Zitron if financing pulls back into a bust. Both are polemicists with incentives, NLW bullish and builder-facing, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype, so the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors, and here they agree the subsidy era is over. Watch the next capex guidance.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Sovereignty and retention are now a GP-data question too

The June story that spooked enterprises — a 30-day retention policy and a government able to switch a model off overnight — lands squarely on NHS ground. Any AI scribe or triage tool a surgery relies on sits with a single vendor whose access can be paused by policy or export control. Worth confirming with your practice where consultation transcripts are processed and stored, what the retention window is, and whether there is a fallback if the service goes dark for a week.

### If NLW is right, cheaper local models could reach primary care

The month's real shift was open-weight and post-trained models becoming a serious boardroom option. If that holds, the plausible NHS dividend is scribe and coding tools that run on local or UK-hosted infrastructure at lower cost, easing the data-residency worries that slow procurement. For a scribe user that would mean less dependence on one US lab and fewer information-governance objections.

### If Zitron is right, budget for prices to rise, not fall

If the buildout is a bubble whose costs are only now biting, today's affordable subscriptions are underpriced and will climb, or vendors will fail. Prudent practice planning treats the current AI scribe deal as provisional: keep the manual workflow usable, avoid single-vendor lock-in, and don't restructure clinics around a tool whose pricing may jump. The 6.4 hours a week of "bot sitting" is also a real cost to weigh against any time saved.

## NLW's Takes

### June was one of the most significant months in post-ChatGPT AI history

NLW frames May and June as a matched pair telling the same story from different angles — the shift from the AI subsidy era to the token scarcity era becoming real, then getting supercharged by a stunning new model and government intervention.

### Most companies aren't even close to needing token efficiency

NLW notes it's reductive to think every company was throwing frontier models at every workload — most haven't adopted AI enough to worry about efficiency, consuming a "vanishingly small" portion of the intelligence they'll ultimately use.

### Fable 5 killed the "completion energy" problem

NLW's best non-technical description: earlier coding models lowered the activation energy to start projects but not the completion energy to finish them, leaving him stuck at 80-90%. Fable 5 was the first model that made finishing big projects feel insignificant — the new AI Daily Brief website was one result.

### Now there are two reasons to diversify: cost AND sovereignty

The Fable pause became the second major reason, after cost, for companies to look at alternatives to closed frontier models. Companies now had both a cost and a sovereignty dimension for diversifying their architecture away from just OpenAI or Anthropic.

### Local AI became a serious boardroom question for the first time

NLW says that for the first time since he's done the show, local AI and open-weight models became a genuine enterprise boardroom conversation worldwide — with firms reevaluating their policies on running models locally.

### The summer slowdown is your window to race ahead

With Fable 5 back, NLW sees a unique July-August opportunity: big chunks of the corporate world turn off for the summer, so anyone who instead uses the time to see what this new class of models can do can significantly increase their value.
