## 9 AI Techniques You Probably Haven't Tried

_A personalized cancer vaccine clears phase III and reignites the fight over what counts as 'AI' — then NLW switches into operator mode with nine techniques, from live voice mode to team agents to local Qwen, that even power users probably haven't tried yet._

## The One Idea

### AI is changing faster than your habits — nine techniques to catch up before fall.

The thing that makes AI exciting is the thing that makes it hard: it changes constantly, and even power users settle into comfortable workflows while the frontier moves on. With back-to-school season approaching, this episode aggregates what people are learning in public — live voice mode, teaching agents by letting them watch, writing skills, /design, multiplayer AI, GrokBot, local models, and two-word prompts — as a menu for your next experimental day.

## The Other Side

Nothing newer had been published, so the counter-source is Monday's essay again — but the half of it that bites on a nine-techniques menu is a different half. Most of the tools on this list sit on one company that, by Zitron's arithmetic, carries over $800bn of compute commitments through 2030, needs $100–200bn a year in fresh capital, and lost $20.9bn on $13.07bn of revenue in 2025; his conclusion is that "at some point, OpenAI will simply not be able to pay its bills". What he sketches for users rather than investors is customers handed "API keys that flow to Microsoft AI Foundry", the free ChatGPT tier stripped back, and no further training of the models people had built their week around. He is not arguing that voice mode or computer history fail to work — he is arguing the entity underwriting them may not be there to keep them working.

## The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is durability: whether tools you build habits on this autumn still exist, at the same price and capability, in two years. Both accept the features work now and that the firms shipping them lose money at scale. The empirical question that resolves is whether OpenAI closes its next raise, and the observable that settles it is the terms — a round at or above the $852bn mark by roughly March 2027, versus a down round, a Microsoft absorption, or a quiet rescue. The residue is worldview: Whittemore treats capability as the thing that compounds and financing as someone else's problem; Zitron treats financing as what determines whether capability survives. Whittemore is vindicated if the money arrives and these techniques become ordinary; Zitron if a funding failure strands them. Both are polemicists with incentives — Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — and the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: none of this is yet paid for by the people using it. Watch the next raise, and its price.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### The melanoma vaccine result is the one thing here a patient may actually raise with you

Moderna and Merck's personalised mRNA vaccine extended remission in a phase III trial of more than 1,100 patients with advanced melanoma, and lung cancer trials are running. Two practical points for the surgery. First, the mechanism is worth being able to explain in a sentence: the tumour is sequenced, its mutations identified, and an mRNA vaccine made to that individual's cancer — it is adjuvant to existing immunotherapy, not a replacement for it, and not a preventive vaccine in the sense patients will assume. Second, it is not available here, and a phase III readout is the start of a regulatory and manufacturing road, not the end of one. The honest answer to 'should I be asking for this?' is that it is genuinely encouraging, that it applies to a specific advanced-melanoma population under specialist care, and that any route to it now is through their oncology team or a trial. Worth reading the eventual paper rather than the press release.

### Ambient voice is the next scribe question — 'let the AI watch you work' is not

Two of the nine techniques matter to a practice that already uses a scribe, and they pull in opposite directions. Ambient voice mode — speaking to the assistant continuously rather than clicking to dictate — is a plausible extension of what a scribe already does, and if a supplier offers it the governance questions are the familiar ones: what is recorded between consultations, when does the microphone stop, and can a patient decline it as easily as they can decline the scribe. The other technique, letting the tool keep a timeline of your screen activity and turn it into repeatable processes, is a straightforward no on a clinical desktop. A general-purpose product logging what is on a GP's screen is logging identifiable patient data into a consumer service with no DPIA, no data processing agreement and no lawful basis. It is a good idea in a marketing department and an information governance incident in a consulting room.

### If Zitron's right, don't let the workflow live only in the tool

NLW's framing is that habits should be rebuilt every few months around whatever is newest. Zitron's counter-argument this week is that the company underwriting several of these tools may not be able to pay its bills, and that users could be moved onto other providers or lose the free tier. Both readings point to the same practical discipline for a practice. If a way of working is worth adopting — a letter-writing standard encoded as a reusable instruction, a triage summary format, a coding prompt — write it down somewhere the practice owns, as plain text, so it can be moved to another vendor in an afternoon. That costs nothing if NLW is right and saves the winter if Zitron is. The same applies to the scribe: know what the fallback is before you need it, because the answer 'we go back to typing' is only tolerable if someone has checked the appointment book can absorb it.

## NLW's Takes

### Call it machine learning if you want — just don't dismiss it

Critics are right that this isn't scientists plugging results into ChatGPT — it's advanced ML closer to AlphaFold, and the brilliant human scientists shouldn't be erased by attributing the breakthrough to 'AI.' But it would also be dismissive to call it hype: the AI era's investment in compute, research, and talent fed this, and the market noticed — Moderna jumped 70%, then 125%, within hours.

### OpenAI is fighting on two frontiers at once

They're competing at the state of the art with 5.6 Sol and the Astra model to come — but since Luna launched, they're also clearly looking behind them at the Chinese open-weight models and refusing to surrender that ground. As users mature in matching power levels to tasks, expect much more competition on the efficiency frontier, not just the capability frontier.

### The meta-technique: steal from people experimenting in public

This episode isn't NLW's own testing — it's an aggregation of what others are sharing, because one of the things that makes AI so cool is that people learn in public. For as cesspool-y as they can be, if you're not watching the AI conversation on X and LinkedIn, you're missing a lot of free R&D.

### Multiplayer AI is going to be the big trend of the fall

Since Open Claw, we've run agents in single-player mode — personal chiefs of staff and research agents working for one person. But work is collaborative, full of handoffs and shared context, and the big opportunity is agentic tools that live where teams intersect rather than where individuals work alone.
