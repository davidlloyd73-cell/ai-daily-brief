## How to Decide What Work AI Should Do for You: The AI Deputization Audit

_Two new features — GrokBot's teach-a-task and ChatGPT's Computer History — mark the moment the AI bottleneck officially moved from capability to context. But once your AI can learn how you work, you still have to decide what work it should actually do. NLW proposes a scoring system: the AI deputization audit._

Source: https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-14

Audio: https://davids-ai-brief.netlify.app/audio/2026-08-14.mp3

## The One Idea

### AI's bottleneck has moved from capability to context — now the question is what you deputize.

GrokBot lets you teach a task by recording yourself doing it; ChatGPT's Computer History watches how you work and learns over time. Deliberate demonstration and ambient observation both solve the same problem: models are capable enough, they just don't know your work. What's left is a decision framework — score each recurring process on frequency, teachability, checkability, stakes, and how much it really needs to be you. Deputize the 8-10s, defend the 0-3s, and duet on everything in the middle, which is where most knowledge work still lives.

## The Other Side

NLW's lead is that models are now capable enough, the missing ingredient is context, and the sensible response is an audit that hands recurring work over. Zitron's nearest published argument attacks the arithmetic underneath that audit: he says nobody can measure the return on AI, or even "create a standard measurement of the cost of a task", because outputs are hallucination-prone and every harness behaves differently. His evidence is billing rather than benchmarks — GitHub Copilot users burning a third of a month's credits in a few hours once token pricing arrived, Uber's COO saying the spend was getting harder to justify, Walmart and Amazon telling staff to stop using AI for its own sake. And on the point NLW calls checkability, Zitron's position is that the vigilance never ends: a model with no ability to learn outside training can go wrong unpredictably on any given run, so the human stays in the loop indefinitely.

Source: Ed Zitron, "AI Doesn't Have ROI", 2 June 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-doesnt-have-roi/

## The Truth Beneath

Both men orbit one variable: the cost of checking work you have handed over, and whether that cost falls as the machine learns how you work. They agree on more than the framing admits — that most delegable work is dull and recurring, that output must be verified, and that users have been shielded from what a task really costs. The empirical question is whether verification and cost per task fall as observed context accumulates; the observable that settles it is organisations publishing before-and-after error rates and per-task spend on a specific deputized process, rather than adoption anecdotes. The residue is worldview: Whittemore reads a solvable context gap, Zitron an unmeasurable product sold on obscured pricing. Whittemore is vindicated if recurring processes run on spot checks at stable cost; Zitron if checking still takes as long as doing and bills keep surprising people. Both are polemicists with incentives — Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — and the signal is their shared premise that checkability decides everything. Watch for the first honest published per-task cost and error rate on a deputized workflow.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### The deputization audit fits a surgery well — until you reach the checkability column

Inventory the recurring work of a practice and the list writes itself: coding and filing incoming letters, results acknowledgement, medication query triage, recall and QOF chasing, drafting routine referrals, insurance and DWP report first drafts, the endless summarising. Score those on NLW's five dimensions and most sit in his middle tier, because the third criterion — how long checking takes against doing — behaves differently in clinical work. A referral letter that reads well but has the wrong laterality costs more to miss than it saved to draft, and the responsible clinician signs it either way, so the check cannot be delegated with the task. That is the honest reason a scribe has been transformative while more ambitious hand-offs have not: the scribe produces something I was going to read carefully in any case.

### Ambient observation is the wrong shape for a consulting-room desktop

A tool that watches everything you do across every application, and learns from it, is a very different information governance proposition in a surgery than in a marketing department. The screen holds other patients' records, open pathology, safeguarding notes and correspondence about third parties, and a continuous capture of interaction events is still processing of special category data that needs a DPIA, a clear lawful basis, and DCB0129/0160 clinical safety assurance from the supplier. Deliberate demonstration — you decide to record one workflow, on synthetic or your own test data — is much easier to justify than passive observation, and it is the option I would ask about first. If NLW is right that ambient context is where the real gains are, then practices will need suppliers to answer the governance questions properly rather than shipping a consumer feature into the NHS by the back door.

### Cost the experiment at the price it will be, not the price it is

NLW closes by suggesting the price of these agent tools will come down as access widens. Zitron's counter is that buyers have never seen the true cost, and that the arrival of real billing produced spending caps rather than enthusiasm. For a practice deciding whether to build a workflow around any of this, the two positions converge on the same prudent question to a supplier: what does this cost per unit of work, and what happens to that figure when the current pricing ends? A scribe subscription that is affordable at one price and unaffordable at three times it is a workflow risk as much as a budget one, and the mitigation is the dull one — do not build a process you could not run without the tool for a fortnight.

## NLW's Takes

### The 'model race' is branching into multiple races

There's still a race for the frontier — but also races for distribution, harnesses, and revenue. Within models alone, Google is betting speed is a dimension people will pay attention to, and the massive efficiency boost also makes 3.7 Flash a big upgrade for Gemini Spark, Google's personal agent.

### Nine times out of ten, the Occam's razor explanation for personnel moves is personal

There's an outsized appetite right now to read every executive shift as a tea leaf for major problems inside OpenAI, and analyzing from outside is fraught. Still, the market is noticing — turnover is being flagged as a pre-IPO problem, though with the listing now delayed to next year, there's plenty of time to build another narrative.

### What changed isn't just privacy norms — it's the value proposition

Microsoft pitched Recall as solving 'finding something you've seen before on your PC' — not a big enough problem to risk the privacy trade. An AI agent that actually does your work for you is a much different value proposition, and now there's real choice in how agents get the context they need.

### Deputize, don't automate

The word choice is intentional: deputizing AI to go do something in your stead is a different relationship than automating a task away and never thinking about it again. That framing shapes the whole audit.

### The biggest barrier is carving out time to work differently

Even highly AI-forward people struggle here — especially high-productivity workers whose workflows are so dialed in that changing them feels like a short-term waste of time, even when it would save a lot of time in the long run. The experiments are worth it; watch for GrokBot's cost to come down as access expands.
