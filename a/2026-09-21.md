# David's AI Daily Brief

**Monday 21 September 2026 · 27 min listen**

## The State of the AI Debate

_The next era of the AI debate is here: Trump wants an AI Force and a rebrand, governors want kill switches and data center restrictions, and Anthropic's IPO slips into November under a safety-shaped cloud. Behind all of it sits the question that backstops every US policy fight — what will China actually agree to? With Xi Jinping arriving in Washington this week, we might finally get an answer._

## The One Idea

### Every US AI policy fight now runs through Beijing.

Kill switches, pauses, pacing the frontier — all of it is backstopped by assumptions about what China will or won't do, because if China isn't on board, these policies just cut the US out of the race. This week's Xi visit is the first real chance to test those assumptions directly. And what we already know suggests the two AI superpowers aren't even worried about the same things: America debates existential risk while an AI build-out props up its economy; China worries about espionage and party control while getting no economic lift from AI at all.

## The Other Side

NLW treats the first cracks in data centre debt as a signal worth watching rather than a verdict. Jane Street-linked bonds have moved from 8.9% to 11.3%, Oracle-linked debt is quoted at 89 cents, and Meta's junk deal was heavily oversubscribed. Investors are still lending, he says, they just want more premium, and bond spreads are where to look if the AI trade turns. Zitron's Hater's Guide to AI Debt, published three days earlier (the free portion is summarised here; the rest is paywalled), argues the cracks are structural. In his telling Oracle, Google and Amazon have gone cash-flow negative, with Meta close behind, as capital spending eats almost every dollar the hyperscalers bring in. Neoclouds' revenues are 'a footnote' to their outgoings, and more than $500 billion of AI-related debt has already been raised in 2026 through bonds, special-purpose vehicles, convertibles and private credit, often from the same few lenders. And the need is growing just as money gets dearer: Goldman expects $400 billion of hyperscaler bonds in 2027, interest rates are rising for everyone, and NVIDIA has raised prices 15% on DRAM costs. His summary is that 'hundreds of billions of dollars are being invested to make single-digit billions of dollars maybe, some day'.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Premium: The Hater's Guide To AI Debt (Part 1)", 18 September 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/premium-the-haters-guide-to-ai-debt-part-1/

## The Truth Beneath

Both men keep coming back to one question: can the AI build-out keep borrowing on terms it can afford? Both accept the facts. Debt, not cash, is now funding the data centres. Rates are rising. The weakest, junk-rated borrowers are paying much more. Anthropic's route to 'profitability' leaves out training costs and stock compensation. NLW's own guests warn that new data centre deals will add structural costs. The question that will settle it is whether the next round of data centre financing clears, and at what price. The thing to look for is spreads on new junk-rated and project-financed data centre deals over the coming quarter, plus whether Oracle keeps its investment-grade rating. What remains is worldview. NLW sees a normal credit market separating the haves from the have-nots while demand stays real. Zitron sees an industry that can only keep going by borrowing more, more expensively, against revenue that is not there. NLW is vindicated if investment-grade borrowers keep raising money comfortably and Anthropic's IPO prices well in November. Zitron is vindicated if a data centre deal fails to refinance or Oracle is downgraded. Both are polemicists with incentives, NLW bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype. The signal is that both now point to the bond market, not share prices, as the place the truth will show first. Watch Oracle's credit rating.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Kill switches are a familiar idea: every clinical tool needs a stop procedure

Newsom's kill-switch order and the federal bills are about frontier models, but the principle already belongs in every practice using AI. For each tool, whether scribe, triage or document workflow, someone should know how to switch it off, what the fallback process is, and who is told. That is business continuity planning, the same as for a clinical system outage. If the answer to 'what happens if we turn the scribe off tomorrow' is that nobody remembers how to do the notes by hand, that is a risk worth writing down.

### Supplier finances are now a clinical safety question

The first cracks in data centre debt and the asterisks on Anthropic's profitability are a reminder that the AI stack beneath NHS tools rests on businesses under financial strain. If borrowing costs rise and cheap compute disappears, suppliers may raise prices, change the model underneath without warning, or fail. Practices and ICBs choosing AI tools should consider supplier viability the way they would for any clinical system. Is there a contract exit route? Are notes and data returned in a usable format? Will you be told when the underlying model changes?

### The US-China AI story reaches general practice through cyber and data

The live US-China issue is not extinction but espionage and data security, including the Claude Code backdoor row and military documents uploaded to a chatbot. For the NHS the practical concern is the same one that runs through DSPT: know where your data is processed, avoid consumer AI tools for anything identifiable, and be alert to staff pasting sensitive material into unapproved services. Geopolitics will shape which models NHS suppliers can use, but the basics of data handling sit with the practice.

## NLW's Takes

### Separate the business-model hedge from the conspiracy

There are two different things in that analysis: whether labs worry token prices get competed down and want to use frontier access to do things rather than just sell tokens — and whether pacing the frontier is a vehicle for that strategy. The first is worth taking seriously even if you reject the second entirely.

### If the AI trade rolls over, debt markets will tell you first

For anyone watching for signs the AI trade is turning, this corner of the market — bond spreads, junk-rated data center funding — is where to look, not stock prices.

### Don't laugh off AI Force too fast

Space Force got mocked too, but strip away the nonsense and it consolidated satellite operations across the Army, Air Force, and Navy. Hold aside the X-risk debates: advanced AI guarantees a new era of cybersecurity concerns, and a central body supporting cyber defense across the military might not be a crazy notion.
