## AI Just Made History in the Middle of a Meltdown

_AI may have just cracked the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most storied problems in mathematics, in the same week Jensen Huang was reinstated at Nvidia with the President's thumb on the scale and Anthropic's IPO drew a backlash. Historic capability and a narrative meltdown are arriving at the same time._

## The One Idea

### AI is making history while its narrative melts down.

In basically every way, we're entering a new phase of AI. An Anthropic mathematician working with Claude has claimed a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and the labs are already circling P vs NP. Meanwhile Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia because President Trump personally intervened, which turns a tech-and-China scandal into a potential corruption scandal. Anthropic's record IPO is being cast as a cynical liquidity grab. The fight to return Jensen may be over, but the battle to recover the AI narrative has barely begun.

## The Other Side

Zitron, writing the same day, would say the industry's problem is not its narrative but its finances. He argues the President forced Huang's return to steady the stock market, and that an industry needing that is brittle. He calls Anthropic's flotation "exit liquidity for its founders, staff and VCs", noting the shares have slid back towards the offer price and that many more will become tradeable within six months. He says token spending has plateaued and prices are falling. He does not mention the claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Defective Accelerationism", 24 November 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/defective-accelerationism/

## The Truth Beneath

The variable both men orbit is whether the industry's trouble is one of reputation or of solvency. Both accept as fact that the President intervened in Huang's reinstatement, that Anthropic's shares sit only just above their offer price, and that its founders sold stock. The empirical question is whether the money holds; the observable is whether Anthropic stays above its eighty-three dollar offer price through the end of the lock-up, and what its first accounts show. The residue is worldview: whether a striking result in mathematics tells you anything about a business. For Whittemore to be vindicated, the proof must survive checking and capability gains must turn into revenue while the politics is managed. For Zitron, the shares must break the offer price and funding tighten, whatever the models can do. Both are polemicists with incentives: Whittemore bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype. The signal is that both describe the flotation as widely seen as a sale by insiders. Watch the offer price and the lock-up expiry.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### A result nobody can read

The claimed proof runs to seventeen million lines of machine-checkable code that no mathematician can follow, and its own author says it may be wrong. Medicine will meet the same problem: an answer that is checkable by machine but not understood by any person. For now our rule should hold. If no accountable clinician can explain why a recommendation is right, it is not ready to act on.

### Unverified is unverified

NLW is careful to call this a historic claim and not a settled fact; some already report errors in the code. Zitron ignores it. Treat it as we would an exciting preprint from a company with a product to sell, announced the week after its flotation: interesting, possibly important, and awaiting independent confirmation.

### Models whose reasoning cannot be seen

A smaller item matters more for us. New model designs do their reasoning internally and no longer show a trace of it. For clinical safety that removes one of the few ways to audit why a tool said what it said. When a supplier changes model, ask whether the reasoning is still inspectable. Otherwise this edition has little direct clinical relevance.

## NLW's Takes

### Jensen is back. That doesn't mean it's over

Huang returned to applause and promised to be "a better CEO the second time around." As Sam Altman learned in 2023, things never fully return to normal after a reinstatement, and Jensen's road may be even harder. This is only the opening act.

### Trump turned a China scandal into a corruption scandal

56% of voters this month said AI and data centers were a major factor in their vote, and 90% said the same of corruption, the top issue ahead of cost of living. By intervening so overtly, the president made this about Jensen's ties to Trump as well as his ties to China, and handed Congress fresh ammunition.

### Why this fight has legs: nine or ten reasonable lenses

Executive accountability and double standards, media hit-jobism, cronyism, "number go up" economics, state-capitalist intervention, open vs. closed source. Even people who put national security first disagree: some say chips reaching China help China, others say they hurt it. Any of these can reasonably be the most important to a given person, so the story won't settle.

### The battle to recover the AI narrative has barely begun

Jensen faces months of grueling hearings, Trump has tied himself tightly to the scandal, and the first-ever retail access to a frontier lab is being cast as a cynical liquidity grab. The fight to return Jensen may be over, but the narrative war is just starting.
