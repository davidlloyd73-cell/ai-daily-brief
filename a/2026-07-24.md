## Why AI Hasn’t Increased Unemployment, According to Anthropic

_Anthropic's head of economics makes the case that AI has caused no material rise in unemployment — because it's a skill-biased, labor-augmenting technology, not a replacement one. Plus a headlines run stuffed with the new hot category: model routing, as Stripe circles OpenRouter for $10B._

## The One Idea

### AI hasn't raised unemployment because it augments experts instead of replacing them.

Anthropic economist Peter McCrory argues the US labor market is stable near full employment, and that AI so far behaves as a skill-biased, labor-augmenting technology: it complements domain expertise, relies on humans to steer a still-jagged frontier, and expands what people can do rather than shrinking headcount. He's clear this could change as agents get more capable and as AI starts automating innovation itself — but for now, he doesn't expect unemployment to be noticeably higher a year from now because of AI. NLW's added point: the more the narrative shifts from cost-cutting to augmentation, the more self-reinforcing the good outcome becomes.

## The Other Side

Where McCrory reads a stable labour market and rising productivity as proof that AI is a real, value-creating technology quietly augmenting workers, Zitron denies the premise underneath it: that AI is producing measurable economic value at all. He argues the AI build-out "isn't a result of any actual return on investment — whether that be in purely monetary terms, like revenue or profitability, productivity gains, or anything tangible or measurable," but is instead "the greatest capital misallocation in history," held aloft only because OpenAI has not yet collapsed. On his reading, the very productivity figures McCrory cites are noise dressed up as signal; the honest test is not the unemployment rate but whether the trillions in spending ever convert into durable profit, and he expects they will not.

Source: Ed Zitron, "The OpenAI Bubble", 15 July 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-openai-bubble/

## The Truth Beneath

The single variable both men orbit is whether AI is yet producing real, measurable economic value — the productivity that McCrory says is already visible and Zitron says does not exist. Both accept the plain facts: unemployment sits near 4.2%, no mass AI layoffs have arrived, and capital spending is enormous. The empirical question that will resolve is whether the recent uptick in labour productivity (2% versus 1.6% before the pandemic) is genuinely AI-driven and durable, or a transient blip; the observable that settles it is sustained total-factor productivity growth attributable to AI alongside real firm-level returns, set against whether OpenAI's finances and remaining performance obligations actually hold. The residue is worldview: McCrory reads early numbers as augmentation beginning to show; Zitron reads the same spending as psychosis awaiting its Lehman moment. McCrory is vindicated if productivity growth persists and firms bank real returns without a lab collapsing; Zitron if the gains revert and OpenAI's funding unravels. Both are polemicists with incentives — NLW bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — so the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: both concede AI has not yet produced mass unemployment. Watch the next few quarters of aggregate productivity data, and whether OpenAI's committed spending stays funded.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### What 'augmentation, not replacement' means for a surgery

McCrory's finding — that AI so far complements expertise rather than replacing it, and that people with more domain knowledge get more out of it — maps neatly onto what an AI scribe actually does in a consulting room. The tool drafts the letter or the notes; the clinician still steers, corrects and signs. If McCrory is right, the sensible posture is to use these tools to widen what a GP can get through in a session while keeping the doctor firmly in the loop. If Zitron is right that the underlying economics are hollow, the practical caution is the same one that always applies to a cheap, venture-subsidised tool: do not build your workflow so tightly around it that you are stranded if the price jumps or the supplier folds.

### Junior roles and training are the place to watch

The one soft signal in the essay is weaker hiring for young workers in AI-exposed roles — though McCrory rightly blames a low-hire, low-fire macro market as much as AI. For general practice and the wider NHS the read-across is about training pipelines rather than redundancy: if AI quietly absorbs the routine tasks that junior staff once cut their teeth on, someone has to make sure trainees, HCAs and new GPs still get the reps that build judgement. The augmentation story is only good news if the ladder underneath it is protected.

### A money story with a clinical tail

Most of this edition is macroeconomics, not medicine, and it is fair to say the direct clinical content is thin. The honest takeaway for a practice is disposition, not diagnosis: whether or not you believe the productivity numbers, keep AI as an assistant with a clinician accountable for every output, and avoid long or expensive lock-ins premised on today's generous pricing being permanent.

## NLW's Takes

### Cheaper models could extend the life of AI chips — and de-risk infrastructure

NLW flags the underrated upside of near-frontier small models: because they can run on previous-generation hardware like H100s and A100s, they may meaningfully extend the lifecycle of AI chips and de-risk the massive infrastructure investments.

### Meta's AI-optimism ad is cheesy — and NLW welcomes it anyway

Meta launched a paid ad campaign ('The future is for everyone') betting on AI optimism. NLW, with his ad-production hat on, calls the copy generic and the messenger hard to swallow — but praises any attempt to tell a positive story about AI and hopes Meta blasts it everywhere.

### The augmentation narrative is self-reinforcing

NLW argues the story leaders tell shapes what they do: if the consensus is that AI should cut staff in half — and investors expect it — that's what happens. If the story is about doing more faster, moving into new domains, and launching new products, we get far more of that instead. He knows which is better for the world.
