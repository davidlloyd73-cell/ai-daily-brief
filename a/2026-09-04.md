## How AI Changed This Summer

_A holiday-weekend retrospective on a summer that felt slow and changed everything: Washington became a release gate for frontier models, Kimi K3 delivered another DeepSeek moment, CFOs got their revenge on token budgets, agent management became a real discipline, data centers became midterm politics, and the Hugging Face incident fired the warning shot on agentic cyber risk. And somehow, it all still feels like prelude._

## The One Idea

### The summer AI crossed a threshold — and it all still feels like prelude.

While knowledge workers eased off, AI didn't. A capability line was crossed that turned Washington into a release gate for frontier models. Enterprises hit the wall on token costs and started taking Chinese open weights seriously. Agent management became a field, with harnesses and loops as its first disciplines. Data centers became the most bipartisan issue since beer, and the Hugging Face incident reset our understanding of cyber risk. The capability set, threat profile, opportunities, risks, and political sentiment all changed — and heading into fall, AI is no longer just a topic for technologists.

## The Other Side

Zitron's summer reads the opposite way. Where NLW sees agents 'killing the bubble math' and markets rightly rewarding Microsoft and Nvidia, Zitron pulls together analyst notes from Wells Fargo, Barclays and UBS estimating that seventy per cent or more of the hyperscalers' AI revenue comes from just two customers, OpenAI and Anthropic, both loss-making and both dependent on raising roughly a quarter of a trillion dollars in a single year to pay their compute bills. He argues that outside those two there is no demand remotely close to the capacity being built, that the record one-day gains are capital being extracted from the system rather than evidence of end-customer return, and that there are 'few signs that these companies can be sold to anyone or taken public' — a direct challenge to the two-trillion-dollar IPO NLW calls the prelude to the autumn.

Source: Ed Zitron, "Don't Look Up", 11 August 2026 — https://www.wheresyoured.at/dont-look-up/

## The Truth Beneath

The single variable both men orbit is whether end customers, not investors, are paying for the compute. Both accept as fact that OpenAI and Anthropic are unprofitable, that they account for the bulk of hyperscaler AI growth, and that per-seat pricing never justified the capex. The empirical question that will resolve is whether enterprise spend on agents becomes a large, diversified, self-funding line item; the observable is a hyperscaler breaking out AI revenue by customer, or an S-1 from Anthropic showing gross margin after compute. The worldview residue is whether concentrated, subsidised demand is a bridge or a cliff. NLW is vindicated if the IPO prices near two trillion on disclosed, non-circular revenue and holds. Zitron is vindicated if the IPO slips, or prices on revenue that turns out to be hyperscaler money round-tripped. Both are polemicists with incentives — NLW bullish and anti-doom, Zitron bubble-certain and anti-hype — and the signal is where they agree despite opposite priors: the customer base is dangerously narrow and nobody is disclosing it. Watch the Anthropic S-1's customer-concentration note.

## GP's Corner

_Claude's notes on what today means for UK general practice and the NHS - commentary, not from the podcast itself._

### Two customers hold up the whole shop — and the NHS is neither

NLW's summer says the enterprise wallet for AI is hundreds of pounds per knowledge worker a month, not twenty; Zitron's says almost all of that spend is still two labs paying hyperscalers with borrowed money. For a practice the cautious position is the same either way: NHS primary care is a small, slow customer that neither side is building for. If NLW is right, scribe and triage vendors will keep improving and their prices will fall as OpenAI's 80 per cent cuts trickle down. If Zitron is right, some of those vendors are running on venture subsidy and will vanish or reprice. Either way, ask any supplier which model they sit on, what happens to your data if they fold, and whether the contract lets you leave in ninety days.

### Washington as a release gate has an NHS echo

The edition's most striking line is that frontier models now wait on government before release, and that Fable 5 came back with a thirty-day data-retention rule baked in that made it unusable for many enterprises. That is the world the NHS already lives in: DTAC, DSPT, the clinical safety case under DCB0129 and 0160. A GP contemplating a scribe should read that as reassurance rather than lag — the pace of the frontier matters less than whether the tool you use has passed the boring checks. AT&T's decision to run open-weight models locally for sovereignty reasons is an argument ICBs may soon make about patient data too.

### Agent management, medical examiner's version

NLW says the new discipline is not doing the work but managing agents that do it, and designing the loop that checks their output. Zitron says that mostly produces the feeling of productivity. The medical examiner's office is a useful test case: scrutiny of a death is precisely the kind of judgement neither side wants automated, but the gathering of the record — GP notes, hospital letters, medication lists — is the kind of dull retrieval an agent could do overnight. The Hugging Face incident is the reminder that an agent with access to systems needs the same least-privilege thinking as a locum's smartcard.

## NLW's Takes

### Google's summer: no Gemini 3.5 Pro, two big departures

Gemini 3.5 Pro was targeted for June and has been continuously delayed, presumably because it can't keep pace with the frontier. The bigger Google stories were Jeff Dean's departure and DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis stepping down — widely read as trouble, though NLW argued the positive view: the shakeup might be exactly what Google needs to get back in the race.

### The SaaSpocalypse narrative is quietly unwinding

SaaS companies hammered by the assumption that everyone would vibe-code Salesforce replacements started rebounding this summer, especially after Salesforce's recent earnings. NLW's read: markets are appreciating that for all AI's disruptiveness, institutional inertia slows things down — and existing product categories, like existing employees, have a strong partnership role to play with AI.

### AI stopped being a technologist's topic this summer

The capability set changed, the threat profile changed, the opportunities and risks changed, and political awareness changed. Heading into fall, there is more recognition than ever that AI isn't just for technologists or the B2B crowd — it's going to impact everyone, one way or another.
