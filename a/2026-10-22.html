The AI vs AI Battle for the Internet

Anthropic ships Claude Duet — and it still can't hold the main slot. A PHP vulnerability touching perhaps 70% of the web, a breach at Railway, and millions of AI-driven exploit attempts within hours of disclosure mark what may be the transition between two eras of cybersecurity: one where long-held norms made exploits manageable, and a new one where it's AI versus AI and the winner is whoever coordinates fastest.

The One Idea

Cybersecurity's new era is AI versus AI — and whoever coordinates faster wins.

PHP2Shell was found by a frontier model, disclosed with a defender's window, leaked ten hours early, and exploited millions of times across Cloudflare's network within hours. Add the Railway breach and the OpenCode attack and the shape of the new normal is clear: swarms of cheap, relentless agents on offense versus governed fleets on defense. The old era of manageable exploits and long-held norms is ending; the new one is a coordination race fought at inhuman speeds — and long-term optimism about more secure software doesn't deny how painful the transition is going to be.

GP’s Corner

Claude’s notes on what today means for general practice and the NHS — commentary, not from the podcast itself.

Patch velocity is now a clinical safety issue

The PHP2Shell flaw, which could affect as much as seventy per cent of websites, was exploited millions of times within hours of disclosure. Practice and supplier websites, booking portals and patient-facing forms built on common web software are exposed. Ask your IT supplier and clinical system vendors how fast they patch, and who is responsible out of hours. Under the NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit this is now a live test, not a paper exercise.

Agent keys and shared credentials

The Railway breach exposed encrypted environment variables such as API keys and database credentials. The practical lesson is to keep a list of every service connected to patient data, rotate keys when a supplier reports an incident, and avoid shared logins.

Personal agents and mail

Anthropic's Duet and Meta's Muse connect to email and files. Do not link a personal agent to a work mailbox containing patient identifiable information without a data protection assessment and your practice's approval.

NLW’s Takes

Duet may be part one of a pre-IPO one-two punch

If NLW were a betting man: release your answer to all the personal agents floating around, then give it a new model that makes everyone feel hopelessly behind. In other words, maybe Duet is just prelude to Fable 5.5 heading into Anthropic's IPO.

Years of vague X-risk warnings may have distracted from this

Some rationalists are claiming vindication, but NLW finds that framing a little disingenuous — cyber chaos isn't close to the main thing that crowd warned about, and one could argue the focus on vague existential scenarios distracted from much more current issues like these. The best counterpoint: being concerned about one thing doesn't mean you can't be concerned about another.

Long-term optimism doesn't deny the pain of the transition

The most optimistic take — Martin Casado's — is that AI ultimately makes software far more capable, secure, and reliable, 'but before that, things are going to get much, much worse.' NLW agrees: he's an incredible long-term optimist across AI, but that doesn't deny the potential chaos, pain, and agony of the short and medium term. It's true for jobs, and it's certainly true for cybersecurity. And boy, are we in it now.
